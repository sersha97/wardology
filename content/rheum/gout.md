:::bottom
- **Diagnosis**: needle-shaped, **negatively birefringent** crystals in joint fluid (gold standard).
- **Flare**: NSAID, colchicine or glucocorticoid — choose by kidneys, heart, GI tract and drug interactions.
- **Start ULT** for ≥2 flares/year, tophi, or radiographic damage; consider after the first flare with CKD ≥3, urate >9, or stones.
- **Allopurinol first**, start low (≤100 mg; 50 mg in CKD), titrate to **urate <6 mg/dL**.
- **Do not stop ULT during a flare**; add **prophylaxis for 3–6 months**.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Urate above ~6.8 mg/dL can crystallize in cool, damaged joints (first MTP, midfoot, knee).
- Crystals activate the **NLRP3 inflammasome → IL-1β** → explosive inflammation.
- Most patients **under-excrete** urate (kidneys, diuretics, alcohol, fructose, low-dose aspirin).

## 🔎 Diagnosis

- Aspirate any acutely inflamed joint when possible — **septic arthritis can coexist** (send Gram stain, culture, cell count).
- Serum urate can be **normal during a flare**; recheck 2 weeks after.
- Dual-energy CT or ultrasound "double contour" sign help when aspiration is not possible.

## 💊 Management

| Flare treatment | Avoid when |
|---|---|
| NSAID (naproxen, indomethacin) | CKD, heart failure, PUD, anticoagulation |
| Colchicine (1.2 mg then 0.6 mg 1 h later) | Severe CKD, strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors (clarithromycin, cyclosporine) |
| Prednisone 30–40 mg, taper; intra-articular steroid | Uncontrolled diabetes, infection |

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **HLA-B\*58:01** testing before allopurinol in Southeast Asian and African American patients.
- Allopurinol + **azathioprine** → life-threatening myelosuppression (xanthine oxidase metabolizes azathioprine).
- Febuxostat: boxed warning for cardiovascular death; pegloticase for refractory tophaceous gout (check **G6PD**).
- Switch hydrochlorothiazide to **losartan** (uricosuric) when possible.
:::
