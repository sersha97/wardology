:::bottom
- Acute low back pain **without red flags** → **no imaging**, stay active, heat, NSAID or short muscle relaxant.
- **Red flags** (cancer, fever/IVDU, saddle anesthesia/retention, progressive deficit, fracture risk) → **MRI** (urgent if cauda equina or cord).
- Shoulder: **passive motion normal** → cuff problem; **passive motion lost** → adhesive capsulitis or arthritis.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Most acute back pain is muscular or ligamentous and resolves in weeks.
- **Sciatica** from a herniated disk compresses a nerve root (L5: big toe dorsiflexion; S1: ankle reflex, plantar flexion).
- The rotator cuff tendons (especially **supraspinatus**) wear under the acromion → tendinopathy → tear.

## 🔎 Shoulder by examination

| Finding | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Painful arc 60°–120°, positive Neer/Hawkins | Subacromial impingement / cuff tendinopathy |
| Weakness, positive drop-arm, passive ROM normal | Rotator cuff tear |
| Loss of active **and** passive ROM, external rotation worst, normal X-ray | Adhesive capsulitis |
| Loss of active and passive ROM, joint-space loss on X-ray | Glenohumeral osteoarthritis |
| Bicipital groove tenderness, positive Speed test | Biceps tendinopathy |

## 💊 Management

- Back: chronic pain → exercise, CBT, yoga, tai chi, spinal manipulation; NSAIDs, then duloxetine. Avoid opioids.
- Shoulder: physical therapy first; subacromial or intra-articular glucocorticoid injection for pain; MRI and surgical referral for acute traumatic tears or weakness in young or active patients.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Imaging nonspecific back pain does not improve outcomes and leads to more procedures.
- Cauda equina: saddle anesthesia, urinary **retention** with overflow → emergency MRI and decompression.
- Adhesive capsulitis is common in **diabetes** and hypothyroidism.
:::
