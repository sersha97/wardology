:::bottom
- Age >50 with **new headache, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness or visual symptoms** + high ESR/CRP → **GCA**.
- **Treat first** (prednisone 40–60 mg; IV methylprednisolone 500–1000 mg/day × 3 if vision loss), **then** biopsy or ultrasound within 1–2 weeks.
- Add **tocilizumab** to spare steroids.
- **PMR alone** → prednisone **12.5–25 mg/day**, dramatic response within days.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Granulomatous inflammation of medium and **large arteries** (temporal, ophthalmic, aorta and its branches).
- Ischemia of the **posterior ciliary arteries → anterior ischemic optic neuropathy** → sudden, often permanent blindness.
- PMR is inflammation of bursae and tendon sheaths around the shoulders and hips; same age group, same HLA background.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| Test | Notes |
|---|---|
| ESR, CRP | Almost always elevated; normal values make GCA unlikely |
| Temporal artery biopsy | Long segment (≥1 cm); skip lesions → can be falsely negative |
| Temporal artery ultrasound | "Halo" sign; preferred first test in many centers |
| CT/MR angiography or PET | Large-vessel GCA (arm claudication, unequal BP, aortic aneurysm) |

## 💊 Management

- Taper glucocorticoids over 12–18 months; add bone protection (calcium, vitamin D, bisphosphonate) and PJP prophylaxis when doses are high for long.
- Long-term: watch for **thoracic aortic aneurysm** (periodic imaging).

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- PMR with **headache or visual symptoms** = treat as GCA dose.
- PMR that does not respond to 15–20 mg prednisone within a week → reconsider the diagnosis (RA, malignancy, infection).
- Subclavian involvement → **unequal arm blood pressures** and arm claudication.
:::
