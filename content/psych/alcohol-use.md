:::bottom
- Screen all adults (AUDIT-C or single question: "How many times in the past year have you had ≥5 drinks [men] / ≥4 [women] in a day?").
- Offer **medication to everyone with moderate–severe AUD**.
- **Naltrexone**: not with opioids or acute hepatitis/liver failure.
- **Acamprosate**: not with CrCl ≤30; safe in liver disease and with opioids.
- Combine with counseling, mutual-help groups and follow-up.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Alcohol releases endogenous opioids → dopamine reward; **naltrexone** blocks the opioid "high."
- Chronic alcohol suppresses glutamate and enhances GABA; withdrawal unmasks glutamate excess → **acamprosate** dampens this protracted withdrawal.

## 💊 Medications

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| Naltrexone 50 mg daily or 380 mg IM monthly | Reduces heavy drinking days |
| Acamprosate 666 mg TID | Maintains abstinence; renal dosing |
| Disulfiram | Aversive; only with supervised dosing |
| Gabapentin, topiramate | Second-line; useful with comorbid pain, insomnia |

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Withdrawal: symptom-triggered benzodiazepines (CIWA-Ar); phenobarbital as an alternative; **thiamine before glucose**.
- Seizures peak at 12–48 hours; delirium tremens at 48–96 hours.
- Alcohol-associated liver disease: abstinence is the most important treatment — offer AUD medication (acamprosate or baclofen if cirrhosis).
:::
