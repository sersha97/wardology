**Wardology — the science of ward medicine** is a free, non-commercial **hospital medicine handbook** for physicians, medical residents and students on inpatient rotations.

## What is in it

- **Clinical reference chapters** organized by organ system: cardiology and electrophysiology, pulmonary and critical care, nephrology, infectious disease, hematology, gastroenterology, endocrinology, neurology, geriatrics and night-call problems.
- For each condition: a short pathophysiology review, **diagnostic criteria**, practical **inpatient management**, a discharge checklist and a **board corner** for ABIM exam review.
- Tools for the wards: clinical formulas, quick reference cards and a guideline watch list.

## Who writes it

Wardology is written by **Serhii Shabalin, MD**, an Internal Medicine resident at a community teaching hospital in New Jersey, trained in Kyiv, and a first-generation physician. It started as a personal handbook for daily ward work and board preparation and is shared so other residents can use it too.

## How it is kept up to date

- Every chapter lists its **sources** (society guidelines and major trials) and a **last-reviewed date**.
- Chapters are reviewed against new guidelines **every quarter**.
- Doses are typical adult doses; always check renal and hepatic adjustment and local protocols.

## Important

Wardology is for **medical education** only. It is **not medical advice** and does not replace clinical judgment, local protocols or specialist input. See the [disclaimer](disclaimer.html).
