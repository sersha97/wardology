:::bottom
- **Intramuscular epinephrine** 0.3–0.5 mg (1 mg/mL) in the **anterolateral thigh** — repeat every 5–15 minutes as needed.
- Lay flat, legs up; IV fluids; oxygen. Antihistamines and steroids are **add-ons**, never first.
- Discharge with **two epinephrine autoinjectors**, an action plan and allergy referral.
- Systemic reaction to a sting → **venom immunotherapy** (cuts future risk to <5%).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- IgE on mast cells and basophils binds the allergen → degranulation → histamine, tryptase, leukotrienes → vasodilation, capillary leak, bronchospasm.
- Epinephrine works on all three: α₁ (vasoconstriction), β₁ (contractility), β₂ (bronchodilation, mast-cell stabilization).

## 🔎 Diagnosis

- Acute onset with skin or mucosal involvement **plus** respiratory compromise, hypotension or GI symptoms — or hypotension/bronchospasm alone after a known allergen.
- **Serum tryptase** within 1–2 hours supports the diagnosis.
- **Biphasic** reactions occur in ~5% — observe after severe reactions.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Patients on **β-blockers** may resist epinephrine → **glucagon** IV.
- ACE inhibitor angioedema is **bradykinin-mediated** — no urticaria, poor response to epinephrine; stop the ACE inhibitor (consider icatibant or FFP).
- Radiocontrast reactions are not IgE-mediated and are not related to shellfish allergy.
:::
