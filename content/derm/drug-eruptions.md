:::bottom
- **Stop the suspected drug** — the most important step in every severe reaction.
- **Morbilliform rash**, no fever or organ involvement → stop drug (or treat through), topical steroids, antihistamines.
- **DRESS** (2–8 weeks; fever, facial edema, eosinophilia, liver/kidney) → systemic steroids with slow taper.
- **SJS/TEN** (4–28 days; painful skin, **mucosal erosions**, detachment) → burn or ICU care.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Most severe drug eruptions are **T-cell–mediated (type IV)** — that is why they appear days to weeks after the first dose, and faster on re-exposure.
- Certain **HLA types** raise risk sharply: HLA-B\*58:01 (allopurinol), HLA-B\*15:02 (carbamazepine in Asian ancestry), HLA-B\*57:01 (abacavir).

## 🔎 Tell them apart

| Reaction | Onset | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Morbilliform exanthem | 4–14 days | Pruritic macules/papules, no mucosa, no organs |
| Urticaria/angioedema | Minutes–hours | IgE-mediated; risk of anaphylaxis |
| **DRESS** | 2–8 weeks | Fever, facial edema, lymphadenopathy, **eosinophilia**, hepatitis, nephritis |
| **SJS/TEN** | 4–28 days | Painful dusky skin, **Nikolsky sign**, ≥2 mucosal sites; SJS <10% BSA, TEN >30% |
| AGEP | 1–3 days | Hundreds of sterile pustules, fever, neutrophilia |
| Fixed drug eruption | Recurs at same site | Dusky round plaque, often lips/genitals (NSAIDs, sulfonamides) |

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Common culprits: **allopurinol, sulfonamides, aromatic anticonvulsants, lamotrigine, NSAIDs (oxicams), nevirapine**.
- After DRESS, watch for **autoimmune thyroiditis** months later.
- Ampicillin/amoxicillin rash in **infectious mononucleosis** is not a true penicillin allergy.
:::
