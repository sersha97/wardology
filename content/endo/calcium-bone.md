:::bottom
- **High calcium + high or "normal" PTH** = primary hyperparathyroidism (or FHH — check urine calcium).
- **High calcium + suppressed PTH** = malignancy (PTHrP, bone metastases), vitamin D excess, granulomas, drugs, immobilization.
- Parathyroidectomy for asymptomatic PHPT if: **Ca >1 above normal, age <50, osteoporosis, eGFR <60, stones**, or hypercalciuria.
- Osteoporosis → **bisphosphonate first**; drug holiday after 5 years oral if lower risk; **never stop denosumab without follow-up therapy**.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- PTH raises calcium three ways: bone resorption, renal calcium reabsorption, and activating vitamin D (→ gut absorption). It also wastes phosphate.
- In **FHH**, the calcium-sensing receptor is set too high — calcium is mildly high, PTH normal, and the kidney **keeps** calcium (low urine calcium). Harmless; surgery does not help.
- Osteoporosis: resorption outpaces formation. Bisphosphonates and denosumab slow osteoclasts; teriparatide, abaloparatide and romosozumab build bone.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

- Screen women **≥65** (and younger postmenopausal women at higher risk) with **DXA**. Osteoporosis = T-score ≤ −2.5, **or** a fragility hip/vertebral fracture regardless of T-score.
- Before treatment: calcium, 25-OH vitamin D, creatinine, (PTH, TSH, celiac serology, SPEP if indicated).

## 💊 Management

| Situation | Therapy |
|---|---|
| Osteoporosis, standard risk | Oral alendronate or risedronate; IV zoledronic acid if intolerant |
| Very high risk (recent fracture, T ≤ −3.0, multiple fractures) | Anabolic first (teriparatide, abaloparatide, romosozumab), then antiresorptive |
| CKD with eGFR <30–35 | Denosumab (watch hypocalcemia) |
| After 5 y oral / 3 y IV, lower risk | Drug holiday 2–3 years |

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Atypical femoral fracture prodrome: **thigh or groin pain** on long-term bisphosphonates → femur radiographs.
- Hypercalcemia of malignancy: IV saline, calcitonin (fast, short), zoledronic acid or denosumab.
- Thiazides raise calcium; lithium can cause PTH-mediated hypercalcemia.
- Romosozumab carries a warning for MI and stroke.
:::
