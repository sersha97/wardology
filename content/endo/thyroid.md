:::bottom
- **TSH first.** Low TSH → check free T₄ (and T₃). High TSH → check free T₄.
- Thyrotoxicosis + **high uptake** = gland **making** hormone (Graves, toxic nodule). **Low uptake** = **leaking** or taken hormone (thyroiditis, exogenous).
- Thionamides and radioiodine only help **high-uptake** disease.
- Thyroiditis → **β-blocker** (+ NSAID or prednisone if painful).
- Hypothyroidism → **levothyroxine**, recheck TSH in 6–8 weeks.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- The pituitary watches free T₄: too much hormone → TSH falls; too little → TSH rises. TSH is the most sensitive test of thyroid function **if the pituitary is normal**.
- **Graves disease**: antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor → diffuse goiter, eye disease, high uptake.
- **Thyroiditis** (subacute, painless, postpartum, drug-induced): inflammation breaks follicles → stored hormone leaks out (thyrotoxic) → stores run out (hypothyroid) → most recover.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| Pattern | Think |
|---|---|
| Low TSH, high free T₄, high uptake, diffuse | Graves |
| Low TSH, high free T₄, high uptake, hot nodule(s) | Toxic adenoma / multinodular goiter |
| Low TSH, high free T₄, **low uptake**, tender gland, high ESR | Subacute (de Quervain) thyroiditis |
| Low TSH, high free T₄, low uptake, **low thyroglobulin** | Exogenous thyroid hormone |
| High TSH, low free T₄, TPO antibodies | Hashimoto |
| High TSH, normal free T₄ | Subclinical hypothyroidism |

## 💊 Management

- **Graves**: methimazole (propylthiouracil in first trimester), radioiodine, or thyroidectomy. β-blocker for symptoms.
- **Subacute thyroiditis**: propranolol + NSAID; prednisone if severe.
- **Hypothyroidism**: levothyroxine ~1.6 µg/kg/day (start 25–50 µg in older or cardiac patients). Take on an empty stomach, apart from iron, calcium and PPIs.
- **Subclinical hypothyroidism**: treat if TSH >10, pregnancy or trying to conceive, or symptoms with positive antibodies.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **Pregnancy**: raise levothyroxine dose by ~25%–30% as soon as pregnancy is confirmed.
- **Amiodarone** can cause hypo- or hyperthyroidism (type 1 = iodine-driven, type 2 = destructive thyroiditis).
- Sick euthyroid: low T₃ (± low T₄) with normal or low TSH in acute illness — do not treat; recheck after recovery.
- Biotin supplements can falsely alter thyroid immunoassays.
:::
