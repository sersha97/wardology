:::bottom
- Anisocoria **worse in the dark** → the **small** pupil is abnormal (Horner). **Worse in the light** → the **large** pupil is abnormal (CN III, Adie, drugs).
- **Painful Horner** (neck pain) → **CTA/MRA** for **carotid dissection**.
- **Pupil-involving CN III palsy** → **CTA** for **PComm aneurysm**.
- Sudden painless monocular loss with **cherry-red spot** = **CRAO** → **stroke center now**.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Sympathetics dilate the pupil and lift the lid (Müller muscle); parasympathetics (with CN III) constrict.
- Compressive CN III lesions hit the **outside** pupillary fibers first → dilated pupil; ischemic (diabetic) palsies usually spare the pupil.

## 🔎 Anisocoria

| Finding | Cause | Next |
|---|---|---|
| Ptosis + miosis, dilation lag | **Horner** | Acute/painful → CTA neck; chronic + smoker → chest imaging (Pancoast) |
| Dilated pupil, "down and out" eye, ptosis | **CN III palsy** | **Emergency CTA** if pupil involved |
| Dilated, slow tonic constriction, young woman | Adie pupil | Benign |
| Fixed dilated, no other signs | Pharmacologic (scopolamine patch) | Pilocarpine test |

## 🔎 Sudden painless vision loss

| Cause | Fundus |
|---|---|
| **CRAO** | Pale retina, **cherry-red spot**, emboli |
| CRVO | "Blood and thunder" hemorrhages |
| Retinal detachment | Curtain, floaters, flashes |
| **Arteritic AION (GCA)** | Pale swollen disc; age >50, high ESR → **steroids now** |
| Vitreous hemorrhage | Poor view (diabetes) |

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **Amaurosis fugax** = TIA of the eye → urgent carotid imaging, ECG, echo.
- Optic neuritis: painful with eye movement, RAPD, young woman → MRI (MS).
:::
