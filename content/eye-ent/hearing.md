:::bottom
- **Weber** lateralizes **toward** a conductive loss, **away** from a sensorineural loss.
- **Rinne negative** (bone > air) = conductive loss in that ear.
- **Sudden sensorineural loss** (≤72 h) → **audiometry + oral steroids within 2 weeks** + **MRI** (vestibular schwannoma).
- **Asymmetric** sensorineural loss or unilateral tinnitus → MRI of the internal auditory canals.
:::

## 🔎 Conductive vs sensorineural

| | Conductive | Sensorineural |
|---|---|---|
| Weber | → bad ear | → good ear |
| Rinne (bad ear) | BC > AC (negative) | AC > BC (positive) |
| Causes | Cerumen, otitis media/effusion, otosclerosis, perforation | Presbycusis, noise, ototoxic drugs, Ménière, schwannoma, SSNHL |

## 💊 Ototoxic drugs

- Aminoglycosides, **cisplatin**, loop diuretics (high-dose IV), high-dose aspirin (reversible tinnitus), vancomycin with aminoglycosides.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **Presbycusis**: bilateral high-frequency loss → hearing aids (reduce social isolation; may slow cognitive decline).
- **Ménière**: episodic vertigo (hours) + fluctuating low-frequency loss + tinnitus + aural fullness.
- Otosclerosis: young adult, conductive loss, family history.
:::
