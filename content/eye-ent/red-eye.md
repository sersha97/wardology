:::bottom
- **Red flags**: reduced vision, severe pain, photophobia, abnormal pupil, corneal haze or opacity, contact-lens wearer → **same-day ophthalmology**.
- **Acute angle closure**: halos, hard eye, cloudy cornea, mid-dilated fixed pupil → lower pressure now (timolol, apraclonidine, acetazolamide, then pilocarpine) → **laser iridotomy**.
- **Conjunctivitis**: normal vision, no pain, normal pupil — viral (watery), bacterial (purulent), allergic (itch).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Angle closure: the iris blocks aqueous outflow at the trabecular meshwork — triggered by **pupil dilation** (darkness, anticholinergics, sympathomimetics).
- Redness **around the cornea** (ciliary flush) signals deeper disease (keratitis, uveitis, angle closure); redness that is diffuse and spares the limbus usually means conjunctivitis.

## 🔎 Differential

| Condition | Vision | Pain | Pupil | Clue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Viral conjunctivitis | Normal | Gritty | Normal | Watery, preauricular node, contagious |
| Bacterial conjunctivitis | Normal | Mild | Normal | Purulent; gonococcal = hyperacute |
| Anterior uveitis | Reduced | Ache, photophobia | **Small**, irregular | HLA-B27 disease, sarcoidosis |
| Keratitis | Reduced | Severe | Normal | Contact lenses, corneal infiltrate |
| Angle-closure glaucoma | Reduced | Severe, headache, vomiting | **Mid-dilated, fixed** | Halos, hard eye |
| Subconjunctival hemorrhage | Normal | None | Normal | Flat red patch; check BP and anticoagulation |

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Never prescribe **topical steroids** for a red eye without slit-lamp examination (herpes keratitis worsens, glaucoma, cataract).
- Contact-lens wearer with a red painful eye → **Pseudomonas** keratitis → urgent ophthalmology, fluoroquinolone drops.
- Topical anesthetics are for examination only — never prescribe them.
:::
