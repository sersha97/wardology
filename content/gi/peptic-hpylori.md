:::bottom
- Test for *H. pylori* with **urea breath test, stool antigen or biopsy** — serology only proves past exposure.
- First-line: **bismuth quadruple therapy for 14 days** (unless clarithromycin susceptibility is known).
- **Test of cure for everyone**: ≥4 weeks after antibiotics, ≥1–2 weeks off PPI.
- The two big ulcer causes are ***H. pylori* and NSAIDs**.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- *H. pylori* lives under gastric mucus, makes urease (ammonia buffers acid) and drives inflammation → duodenal ulcer, gastric ulcer, **gastric adenocarcinoma** and **MALT lymphoma**.
- NSAIDs block COX-1 → less protective prostaglandin, less mucus and bicarbonate.

## 🔎 Who to test

- Active or past peptic ulcer, dyspepsia under age 60, gastric MALT lymphoma, before long-term NSAID or aspirin in high-risk patients, unexplained iron deficiency, ITP.
- Dyspepsia **≥60** → endoscopy; younger → test and treat *H. pylori*, then PPI trial.

## 💊 Treatment

| Regimen (14 days) | Components |
|---|---|
| **Bismuth quadruple** (preferred) | PPI + bismuth + tetracycline + metronidazole |
| Vonoprazan dual | Vonoprazan + amoxicillin |
| Clarithromycin triple | Only if strain is known susceptible |

- Failure → avoid repeating clarithromycin or levofloxacin; consider susceptibility testing.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **Gastric** ulcers often need repeat endoscopy to confirm healing and exclude cancer; uncomplicated duodenal ulcers do not.
- Refractory, multiple or distal ulcers with diarrhea → **Zollinger-Ellison** (fasting gastrin off PPI).
- Treat household members? No — test only if symptomatic or high-risk for gastric cancer.
:::
