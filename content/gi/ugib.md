:::bottom
- **Two large-bore IVs**, type & cross, **restrictive transfusion: Hb <7** (<8 with cardiovascular disease).
- **Glasgow-Blatchford ≤1** → can be outpatient.
- **Non-variceal:** IV **PPI** (pantoprazole 80 mg IV, then 40 mg BID) → **EGD within 24 h**.
- **Cirrhosis / suspected varices:** **octreotide** + **ceftriaxone 1 g/day** + **EGD within 12 h** with band ligation. Avoid overtransfusion (raises portal pressure).
- **Erythromycin 250 mg IV 30–120 min before EGD** clears the stomach.
- Reverse anticoagulation for life-threatening bleeding.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Upper GI = **above the ligament of Treitz**. Most common: **peptic ulcer** (*H. pylori*, NSAIDs, aspirin), then **varices**, esophagitis, Mallory-Weiss, Dieulafoy, tumor, angiodysplasia.
- **Varices:** portal pressure (HVPG) >10–12 mmHg → collaterals → thin-walled veins rupture. More volume = more pressure = more bleeding.
- BUN rises because blood protein is absorbed in the small bowel.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| Glasgow-Blatchford components | |
|---|---|
| BUN | ↑ points |
| Hb (sex-specific) | ↓ points |
| SBP | <110 ↑ |
| HR ≥100, melena, syncope, liver disease, HF | +1–2 each |
| **Score 0–1** | Very low risk → outpatient EGD |

- Melena = ~>50–100 mL of upper bleed. **Bright red blood per rectum + shock** can be a brisk upper bleed (check BUN, consider EGD first).
- **Forrest classification** at EGD: Ia spurting, Ib oozing, IIa visible vessel, IIb adherent clot → endoscopic therapy; IIc flat spot, III clean base → low risk.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Non-variceal
- IV PPI before EGD (downstages lesions).
- After endoscopic therapy of high-risk ulcer → **high-dose PPI for 3 days** (continuous or intermittent bolus), then PO BID to 2 weeks, then daily.
- **Test for *H. pylori*** and treat. Stop NSAIDs.
- **Aspirin for secondary prevention:** restart as soon as hemostasis is secured (ideally within days).
- Rebleed → repeat EGD → IR embolization → surgery.
=== Variceal
- **Octreotide** 50 mcg IV bolus → 50 mcg/h × 2–5 days.
- **Ceftriaxone 1 g IV daily × up to 7 days** (prevents infection, rebleeding, death).
- **EGD ≤12 h** with **band ligation**.
- Lactulose to prevent encephalopathy.
- **Pre-emptive TIPS within 72 h** for high risk (Child C 10–13, or Child B >7 with active bleeding at EGD).
- Uncontrolled → balloon tamponade (Minnesota/Blakemore) as bridge → rescue TIPS.
- Gastric varices → cyanoacrylate/BRTO/TIPS.
- Later: **non-selective β-blocker (carvedilol)** + repeat banding.
=== Anticoagulation
| Drug | Reversal for life-threatening bleed |
|---|---|
| Warfarin | **4F-PCC** + vitamin K 10 mg IV |
| Apixaban / rivaroxaban | **4F-PCC** (andexanet withdrawn from US, Dec 2025) |
| Dabigatran | **Idarucizumab** |
| Heparin | Protamine |
| Antiplatelet | Platelets **not** routinely helpful |
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Overtransfusing a variceal bleeder (Hb target 7–8).
- Forgetting **antibiotics** in cirrhosis with GI bleed.
- Holding aspirin "forever" in a stented patient.
- Tranexamic acid — no benefit (HALT-IT), more VTE.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Cirrhosis + hematemesis | Octreotide + ceftriaxone + EGD ≤12 h |
| Ulcer with visible vessel | Endoscopic therapy + high-dose PPI |
| Retching then hematemesis | Mallory-Weiss |
| Hb 7.6, stable, no CAD | No transfusion |
| Aortic graft + "herald" bleed | Aortoenteric fistula → CT angio, surgery |
