:::bottom
- Typical heartburn without alarm features → **8-week PPI trial**, taken 30–60 minutes before breakfast.
- **Alarm features** (dysphagia, weight loss, bleeding, anemia, vomiting) → **endoscopy**.
- Nondysplastic Barrett → PPI + EGD every **3 years (≥3 cm)** or **5 years (<3 cm)**.
- Dysplasia → **endoscopic eradication** (resection + ablation).
- Use the **lowest effective PPI dose**; try to stop when there is no clear indication.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Transient relaxation of the lower esophageal sphincter and a **hiatal hernia** let acid reflux upward.
- Chronic acid injury can turn squamous lining into **intestinal-type columnar** cells (Barrett) — a protective adaptation that can progress to dysplasia and adenocarcinoma.
- Young man with food impaction and atopy → think **eosinophilic esophagitis**, not GERD.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

- No test needed for typical symptoms that respond to a PPI.
- **Endoscopy**: alarm features, PPI failure, or Barrett screening (chronic GERD + ≥3 risk factors: male, >50, White, central obesity, smoking, family history).
- **Ambulatory pH monitoring off PPI**: to confirm GERD before surgery or long-term PPI when endoscopy is normal.

## 💊 Management

- Weight loss, raise head of bed, avoid late meals for nocturnal symptoms.
- PPI once daily → twice daily if partial response.
- Refractory: confirm adherence and timing, then pH testing; consider fundoplication or magnetic sphincter augmentation.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- PPIs are associated with *C. difficile*, hypomagnesemia, and reduced B₁₂ and iron absorption — rarely reasons to stop a needed PPI.
- Extraesophageal symptoms (cough, laryngitis, asthma) alone respond poorly to PPIs.
- Barrett ablation is for **dysplasia**, not for nondysplastic Barrett.
:::
