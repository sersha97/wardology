:::bottom
- Signs: **headache**, vomiting, **falling level of consciousness**, papilledema, **CN VI palsy**. Late: **one blown pupil**, posturing, **Cushing triad** (↑ BP, ↓ HR, irregular breathing).
- **Head of bed 30°**, neck straight (no tight collar/ties), treat pain, fever, seizures.
- **Hyperosmolar therapy now:** **23.4% saline 30 mL** (central line) or **3% saline 250 mL**, **or mannitol 1 g/kg IV**.
- **Brief hyperventilation** (PaCO₂ **30–35**) only as a **bridge** while you arrange definitive care.
- **CT head** + **neurosurgery now** — the definitive fix is often an **EVD**, decompression or evacuation.
- Keep **MAP up** (CPP = MAP − ICP ≥60). Avoid hypotonic fluids, hypoxia, hypotension.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

{{fig:monro-kellie}}

- Normal ICP **5–15 mmHg**; treat if sustained **>22 mmHg**.
- Causes: **mass** (bleed, tumor, abscess), **edema** (stroke, TBI, liver failure, hyponatremia, DKA in young), **hydrocephalus** (blocked CSF), **venous outflow** block (CVT, neck compression), **CO₂ ↑** (vasodilation).
- **Herniation** = brain pushed from one compartment to another:

| Type | What moves | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncal (transtentorial)** | Medial temporal lobe over tentorium | **Ipsilateral blown pupil (CN III)**, then contralateral weakness (sometimes ipsilateral — Kernohan notch), coma |
| **Central** | Diencephalon down | Small reactive pupils → mid-fixed, decorticate → decerebrate |
| **Subfalcine** | Cingulate under falx | Leg weakness (ACA compression) — often silent |
| **Tonsillar** | Cerebellar tonsils into foramen magnum | Neck pain/stiffness, **sudden apnea, cardiac arrest** — avoid LP with posterior fossa mass |
| **Upward** | Cerebellum up | Posterior fossa mass + EVD; vertical gaze palsy |

:::analogy
The skull is a **suitcase that is already full**. You can squeeze in a sock by pushing out air (CSF and blood). After that, every extra sock **bursts the zipper** — the brain is pushed out through the openings it has.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

- **CT head (non-contrast)** first: mass, midline shift, effaced cisterns/sulci, hydrocephalus, blood.
- **Optic nerve sheath diameter** on ultrasound (>5–6 mm suggests ICP >20) — screening tool.
- **ICP monitor / EVD** placed by neurosurgery (EVD also drains CSF).
- **Do not do an LP** when CT shows a mass, shift or obstructive hydrocephalus.
- Labs: **Na**, osm, glucose, coags/platelets (before procedures), ammonia (liver failure), ABG.

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Signs of herniation (blown pupil, posturing, Cushing triad)?
Y: **Airway** (intubate with neuroprotective technique; avoid hypotension) → **head up 30°** → **23.4% NaCl 30 mL** via central line (or 3% 250 mL, or mannitol 1 g/kg) → **hyperventilate to PaCO₂ 30–35** (short term) → **STAT CT + neurosurgery**.
Q: Cause fixable surgically (hematoma, hydrocephalus, cerebellar mass/stroke)?
Y: **Evacuation, EVD, or decompressive craniectomy.**
Q: Tumor or abscess with vasogenic edema?
Y: **Dexamethasone 10 mg IV** then 4 mg q6h (tumor). **Not** for stroke, ICH or TBI.
Q: ICP still high?
Y: Deeper **sedation** (propofol), treat fever (normothermia), seizure control (EEG), repeat osmotherapy (Na target ~145–155), then neuro-ICU tiers (paralysis, barbiturate coma, hypothermia, craniectomy).
:::

:::tabs
=== Hypertonic saline vs mannitol
| | Hypertonic saline | Mannitol |
|---|---|---|
| Dose | 23.4% 30 mL (central) · 3% 250 mL or 2–5 mL/kg (peripheral OK) | 0.5–1 g/kg IV over 5–15 min |
| Good for | **Hypotension / hypovolemia**, hyponatremia | Euvolemic patient, no central line |
| Watch | Na (avoid >160), volume overload, hyperchloremic acidosis | **Hypotension** (osmotic diuresis), AKI if osm gap >20 |
| Monitor | Na q4–6h | Serum osm + **osmolar gap** q6h |
=== Simple bedside measures
- HOB **30°**; head midline; loosen **ETT ties / C-collar**.
- **Normoxia** (SpO₂ >94%), **normocapnia** (PaCO₂ 35–45) once stable.
- **Normothermia**; acetaminophen, cooling.
- **Isotonic fluids only** (no D5W, no 0.45% saline). Na ≥135.
- Treat **pain, agitation**, cough, constipation (straining).
- **Glucose** 140–180.
- Seizure treatment; EEG if unexplained depressed consciousness.
=== Special settings
- **Acute liver failure:** grade 3–4 encephalopathy → intubate, Na 145–155, **ammonia >150–200** = high risk; transplant center.
- **Hyponatremia with seizures/coma:** 3% saline bolus 100–150 mL (see Hyponatremia).
- **Cerebellar stroke/hemorrhage:** watch for hydrocephalus and brainstem compression → **early neurosurgery**.
- **Large MCA infarct (malignant edema)**, age <60: decompressive hemicraniectomy within 48 h saves lives.
:::

:::dose
- **23.4% NaCl:** 30 mL IV over 10–20 min, **central line only**.
- **3% NaCl:** 250 mL IV bolus (peripheral OK for short boluses).
- **Mannitol 20%:** 0.5–1 g/kg IV; repeat 0.25–0.5 g/kg q6h by osm gap.
- **Dexamethasone (tumor edema):** 10 mg IV once, then 4 mg q6h.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Prolonged **hyperventilation** → vasoconstriction → **ischemia**. Bridge only.
- **Hypotonic fluids** or D5W in brain injury.
- **Steroids for stroke, ICH or TBI** (harmful in TBI — CRASH).
- **LP** with a posterior fossa mass → tonsillar herniation.
- Treating ICP but letting **MAP fall** — perfusion (CPP) is what matters.
- Mannitol in a **hypotensive** patient.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Epidural hematoma, now one dilated fixed pupil and contralateral weakness | Uncal herniation → osmotherapy, hyperventilate briefly, **emergent evacuation** |
| Hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respirations | **Cushing triad** (raised ICP) |
| Raised ICP in a hypotensive trauma patient | **Hypertonic saline** (not mannitol) |
| Glioblastoma with vasogenic edema, drowsy | **Dexamethasone** |
| TBI — should we give steroids? | **No** (CRASH: ↑ mortality) |
| CPP formula | MAP − ICP (goal ≥60) |
| Hyperventilation lowers ICP how? | ↓ PaCO₂ → cerebral **vasoconstriction** → ↓ blood volume |
| Large cerebellar infarct, drowsy, hydrocephalus | EVD ± suboccipital decompression |
