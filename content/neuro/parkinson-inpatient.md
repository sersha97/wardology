:::bottom
- **Levodopa is a time-critical drug.** Order it at the patient's **exact home times** (e.g., 06:00, 09:30, 13:00, 16:30) — **not "TID."** Late by 30+ min → rigidity, falls, aspiration.
- **NPO ≠ no levodopa.** Give crushed **IR carbidopa-levodopa** by NG tube, or bridge with a **rotigotine patch**. Ask pharmacy/neurology **today**.
- **Never** give **haloperidol, metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, promethazine, droperidol** or most antipsychotics.
- Nausea → **ondansetron**. Psychosis/agitation → treat cause, then low-dose **quetiapine** (or clozapine/pimavanserin).
- **MAO-B inhibitors** (selegiline, rasagiline, safinamide): avoid **meperidine, tramadol, methadone, dextromethorphan**, linezolid caution → serotonin syndrome.
- Stopping levodopa suddenly → **parkinsonism-hyperpyrexia syndrome** (NMS-like): restart now.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Loss of **dopamine neurons** in the **substantia nigra** → less dopamine in the striatum → **bradykinesia**, **rigidity**, **rest tremor**, later **postural instability**.
- Levodopa is converted to dopamine in the brain. As disease advances, the brain **stores less**, so each dose lasts **shorter** — "**wearing off**." A late dose = sudden "off" state (frozen, rigid, can't swallow).
- Non-motor problems matter in the hospital: **dysphagia**, **orthostatic hypotension**, **constipation**, urinary retention, **delirium/hallucinations**, depression, sleep disorders.

:::analogy
In early Parkinson the brain has a big **water tank** of dopamine: miss a dose and the tank covers it. In late Parkinson the tank is a **leaky cup** — the next dose must arrive on time or the cup runs dry and the patient "freezes."
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis & assessment

### Admission checklist

- **Exact home medication times and doses** (ask the patient/family; they know). Include **ER** formulations, **COMT inhibitors** (entacapone), MAO-B inhibitors, amantadine, dopamine agonists, **devices** (DBS, Duopa pump, apomorphine pump).
- **"On" vs "off"** state now; usual function at home.
- **Swallow screen** (aspiration pneumonia is a leading cause of death).
- **Orthostatic vitals.**
- Cognition, hallucinations, sleep, falls history.
- **DBS** stimulator: **no diathermy**; MRI only per device rules; cardioversion/surgery → notify neurology.

### Parkinsonism — is it Parkinson disease?

| | Clue |
|---|---|
| **Drug-induced** | Antipsychotics, metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, valproate; symmetric, no rest tremor |
| **Lewy body dementia** | Dementia **before or within 1 year** of parkinsonism, fluctuations, visual hallucinations, **severe antipsychotic sensitivity** |
| **Multiple system atrophy** | Early severe **autonomic failure**, cerebellar signs, poor levodopa response |
| **Progressive supranuclear palsy** | **Vertical gaze palsy** (down), early falls backward, axial rigidity |
| **Normal pressure hydrocephalus** | **Gait (magnetic), urinary incontinence, dementia**; large ventricles |
| **Vascular parkinsonism** | Lower-body, gait-predominant, strokes on imaging |

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: On carbidopa-levodopa?
Y: Order at **home times** as "time-critical"; allow **self-administration** if able. Give with water; protein meals may slow absorption.
Q: NPO (surgery, ileus, dysphagia)?
Y: **IR carbidopa-levodopa crushed via NG** (ER tablets cannot be crushed — convert to IR). No enteral route → **rotigotine patch** (approx. 2 mg/24 h per 100 mg levodopa — ask neurology), or apomorphine SC. Resume oral ASAP.
Q: Confused, hallucinating, agitated?
Y: Look for **infection, retention, constipation, pain, drugs** (anticholinergics, opioids, amantadine, dopamine agonists — reduce these **before** levodopa). Non-drug delirium care. If needed: **quetiapine 12.5–25 mg** at night. Never haloperidol.
Q: Nausea/vomiting?
Y: **Ondansetron** (watch QT) · domperidone (not US). Avoid metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, promethazine.
Q: Fever + rigidity + confusion after missed doses?
Y: **Parkinsonism-hyperpyrexia** → restart levodopa (NG), cool, fluids, check CK; ICU if severe.
:::

:::tabs
=== Common hospital problems
- **Falls:** PT early, walker, avoid sedatives; **freezing** may improve with visual/auditory cues.
- **Orthostatic hypotension:** stop alpha-blockers/excess antihypertensives; fluids, salt, compression; **midodrine** or **droxidopa** or **fludrocortisone**.
- **Constipation:** daily regimen (PEG); can impair levodopa absorption.
- **Dysphagia:** SLP; texture changes; time meals with "on" periods; pills in purée if allowed.
- **Urinary retention:** check PVR before anticholinergic bladder drugs.
- **Post-op:** early mobilization; levodopa as soon as possible after surgery; avoid neuraxial-related delays in doses.
=== Drug interactions to know
| Drug | Problem |
|---|---|
| Haloperidol, risperidone, olanzapine, metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, promethazine, droperidol | Block dopamine → severe worsening, NMS-like |
| MAO-B inhibitor + meperidine, tramadol, methadone, dextromethorphan, linezolid, methylene blue | Serotonin syndrome / hypertensive crisis |
| Iron, high-protein meals | Reduce levodopa absorption (space doses) |
| Pyridoxine (high dose, without carbidopa) | Not relevant with carbidopa combos |
| Anticholinergics (diphenhydramine, oxybutynin, benztropine) | Delirium, retention |
| Benzodiazepines | Falls, delirium |
=== Dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole)
- Side effects: sleep attacks, hallucinations, **impulse-control disorders** (gambling, shopping), edema.
- Stopping them suddenly → **dopamine agonist withdrawal** (anxiety, pain, dysphoria). Taper.
:::

:::dose
- **Carbidopa-levodopa IR:** continue home doses and **times**. Crushed IR via NG OK; **CR/ER** cannot be crushed.
- **Rotigotine patch:** start 2–4 mg/24 h (rough conversion; neurology/pharmacy).
- **Quetiapine:** 12.5–25 mg PO at bedtime, titrate slowly.
- **Ondansetron:** 4 mg IV/PO q8h PRN.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- **"Levodopa TID"** with meals on the hospital schedule.
- **NPO after midnight** — and nobody gives the morning levodopa.
- **Haloperidol** for agitation; **metoclopramide** or **prochlorperazine** for nausea; **promethazine** "because it's an antihistamine."
- Blaming all hallucinations on PD — find **infection** and **drugs** first.
- Tramadol or meperidine for pain on a **MAO-B inhibitor**.
- Forgetting the **DBS device** before surgery or MRI.

|---|---|

| PD patient vomiting, antiemetic choice | **Ondansetron** |
| PD patient NPO for ileus, can't take levodopa | **Rotigotine patch** |
