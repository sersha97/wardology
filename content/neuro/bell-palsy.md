:::bottom
- **Sudden (≤72 h) one-sided facial weakness including the forehead** = peripheral CN VII palsy. Most common cause = **Bell's palsy** (idiopathic).
- **Forehead works** (can raise eyebrow) → **upper motor neuron** → think **stroke** → stroke pathway.
- Treat Bell's within **72 h**: **prednisolone 25 mg BID × 10 days** (or prednisone 60 mg × 5 days then taper). Antivirals alone do **not** work; adding **valacyclovir** is optional (severe cases).
- **Protect the eye:** lubricating drops by day, ointment + **tape the lid** at night. Painful red eye → ophthalmology.
- Look for **mimics**: vesicles in ear (**Ramsay Hunt**), **Lyme** (endemic area, bilateral), parotid mass, other cranial nerves, slow onset (>72 h, tumor).
- ~**70–85% recover fully**, most within 3 months. No recovery by 3–4 months → **MRI**.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **CN VII** runs through a long, narrow bony tunnel (**facial canal**). Inflammation (probably **HSV-1 reactivation**) → swelling in a tight space → **compression** and ischemia → weakness.
- The nerve carries: muscles of facial expression · **taste** (front 2/3 of tongue, chorda tympani) · **tears and saliva** · **stapedius** (loud sounds hurt — hyperacusis).
- **Forehead rule:** the upper face gets cortical input from **both** hemispheres; the lower face only from the **opposite** one. Stroke spares the forehead; nerve lesion does not.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

### Exam

- Raise eyebrows, close eyes tight, puff cheeks, show teeth.
- **Bell's phenomenon:** when trying to close the eye, the eyeball rolls **up**, showing white sclera.
- **Ear:** vesicles in the canal or on the auricle → **Ramsay Hunt** (VZV). Look at the **parotid** (mass), **mouth** (vesicles on palate).
- Full cranial nerve and limb exam: anything else abnormal → **not** Bell's.
- Grade with **House-Brackmann** (I normal → VI total paralysis).

### Tests

- **No routine labs or imaging** for typical Bell's.
- **Lyme serology** in endemic areas (or summer/tick exposure), especially if **bilateral**.
- Glucose/A1c (diabetes is a risk factor), pregnancy is a risk factor.
- **MRI with contrast (internal auditory canal/temporal bone):** atypical features, recurrent same side, other nerves, no improvement by 3–4 months.

:::tabs
=== Mimics and red flags
| Clue | Think |
|---|---|
| **Forehead spared**, arm/speech involvement | **Stroke** |
| Vesicles in ear, severe ear pain, hearing loss/vertigo | **Ramsay Hunt** (VZV) → steroids **+ valacyclovir** |
| **Bilateral** facial palsy | **Lyme**, **GBS**, **sarcoid** (uveitis, parotid — Heerfordt), HIV, leukemia |
| Slow onset (>3 weeks), twitching before palsy, recurrent same side | **Tumor** (parotid, schwannoma, cholesteatoma) |
| Otitis media/mastoid pain | Otitis/mastoiditis — ENT |
| Other cranial nerves, numb face | Skull base / brainstem lesion |
| Trauma | Temporal bone fracture |
=== Who gets antivirals?
- **Prednisolone alone** is the evidence-based treatment (Sullivan 2007, Engström 2008).
- **Valacyclovir 1 g TID × 7 days** may be **added** in **severe/complete** palsy (small possible benefit). Never alone.
- **Ramsay Hunt:** steroids + **valacyclovir** (or acyclovir) for everyone.
- Pregnant or diabetic patients: steroids still recommended (watch glucose).
:::

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Forehead spared, or other neuro deficit?
Y: **Not Bell's** — stroke pathway (CT/MRI, stroke code if in window).
N: Peripheral CN VII palsy.
Q: Ear vesicles, ear pain, hearing loss?
Y: **Ramsay Hunt** → prednisolone + **valacyclovir 1 g TID × 7 days**; ENT/audiology.
Q: Onset within 72 h and no contraindication?
Y: **Prednisolone 25 mg BID × 10 days** (or **prednisone 60 mg daily × 5 days, then taper over 5 days**).
Q: Eye closes incompletely?
Y: **Artificial tears** hourly while awake, **lubricating ointment** at night, **tape the eyelid** closed or moisture chamber, sunglasses. Pain/redness/vision change → **urgent ophthalmology**.
Q: Lyme endemic area or bilateral?
Y: Lyme serology → **doxycycline 100 mg BID × 14 days** if positive (steroids debated — discuss).
:::

:::dose
- **Prednisolone:** 25 mg PO BID × 10 days (no taper needed), **or** prednisone 60 mg daily × 5 days → taper.
- **Valacyclovir:** 1 g PO TID × 7 days (Ramsay Hunt; optional add-on in severe Bell's). Renal adjust.
- **Eye:** carboxymethylcellulose drops q1h while awake; ointment at bedtime.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Calling a **stroke** "Bell's palsy" (check the forehead, check the arm).
- Calling **Bell's palsy** a stroke and giving lytics — check the forehead.
- **Antivirals alone** without steroids.
- Forgetting the **eye** → corneal ulcer.
- Missing **Ramsay Hunt** (look in the ear) and **Lyme** (bilateral, summer, endemic).
- Not re-evaluating a palsy that **never improves** — tumor.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Woke up with right face droop, can't wrinkle forehead, can't close eye | Bell's → **prednisolone** within 72 h + eye care |
| Face droop sparing forehead + arm weakness | Stroke |
| Facial palsy + vesicles in ear canal + hearing loss | **Ramsay Hunt** (VZV) → steroids + valacyclovir |
| Bilateral facial palsy, summer, Connecticut, recent rash | **Lyme** → doxycycline |
| Bilateral facial palsy + uveitis + parotid swelling + fever | Sarcoid (Heerfordt syndrome) |
| Bilateral facial weakness + ascending weakness + areflexia | GBS |
| Slowly progressive facial palsy over 2 months with twitching | Tumor → MRI |
| Most important non-drug treatment | **Eye protection** |
