:::bottom
- **Lacune**: pure motor (posterior limb internal capsule), pure sensory (thalamus), ataxic hemiparesis, dysarthria–clumsy hand — **no cortical signs** (no aphasia, neglect, field cut).
- **Lateral medulla (Wallenberg)**: vertigo, ipsilateral Horner + facial pain/temperature loss + ataxia, **contralateral body** pain/temperature loss, hoarseness/dysphagia — vertebral or PICA.
- **Neck pain + Horner or stroke in a young adult** → **cervical artery dissection** → CTA/MRA; antiplatelet or anticoagulant.
- **CVT**: headache, seizures, papilledema, often postpartum/OCP → MRV/CTV → **anticoagulate even if hemorrhagic infarct**.
- **PRES**: headache, seizures, visual loss + **severe BP or calcineurin inhibitor/chemo/eclampsia** → posterior white-matter edema → control BP, stop the drug.
- **ICH on a factor Xa inhibitor** → **andexanet alfa** or **4F-PCC**; on dabigatran → **idarucizumab**; on warfarin → **4F-PCC + IV vitamin K**. SBP target ~140.
:::

## 🗺️ Localize

| Deficit | Where |
|---|---|
| Aphasia, gaze toward lesion, face/arm > leg weakness | Left MCA (cortex) |
| Neglect, anosognosia | Right MCA |
| Leg > arm weakness, abulia | ACA |
| Homonymous hemianopia, ± alexia without agraphia | PCA |
| **Crossed** signs (ipsilateral cranial nerve, contralateral body) | **Brainstem** |
| Pure motor face = arm = leg, no cortical signs | **Lacune** (internal capsule/pons) |

## 🔎 Young stroke / ESUS workup

- **Prolonged cardiac monitoring** (≥30 days or implantable loop recorder) for paroxysmal AF — finds AF in 10%–30%.
- **TEE/bubble study** for PFO (see ACHD chapter), aortic arch atheroma.
- **Vessel imaging** for dissection; thrombophilia testing mainly for venous strokes or young patients without a cause.
- **Symptomatic intracranial atherosclerosis (70%–99%)** → **aspirin + clopidogrel 90 days**, high-intensity statin, SBP <140, exercise — **not stenting** (SAMMPRIS).

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **Thunderclap headache** → noncontrast CT; within 6 hours of onset a normal modern CT essentially excludes SAH; after 6 h → LP (xanthochromia) or CTA.
- CVT with intracerebral hemorrhage is **not** a contraindication to heparin. DOACs are reasonable after initial heparin (ACTION-CVT, RE-SPECT CVT).
- **RCVS** (reversible cerebral vasoconstriction): recurrent thunderclap headaches, serotonergic drugs, postpartum — "string of beads" vessels that resolve.
:::
