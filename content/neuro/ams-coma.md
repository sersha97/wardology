:::bottom
- **Check a fingerstick glucose** — in every confused patient, first.
- **ABCs and vitals**: hypoxia, **hypercapnia** (get a **VBG/ABG**), hypotension, fever, **very high BP**.
- **Pupils + eyes + motor exam** in 60 seconds: asymmetric or focal = **structural** → **CT head now** ± stroke code.
- Treat what is fast and cheap: **dextrose**, **thiamine** (before or with glucose if malnourished/alcohol), **naloxone** (small doses if opioid-dependent), O₂/BiPAP for CO₂.
- Review the **MAR**: opioids, benzos, anticholinergics, gabapentin, **cefepime**, antipsychotics — especially with **AKI**.
- No clear cause → **EEG** for **non-convulsive status**. Fever → think **meningitis/encephalitis** (LP).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Being awake needs two things working: the **reticular activating system (RAS)** in the brainstem (the "on switch") and **both cerebral hemispheres** (the "content").
- Coma happens with: (1) a **brainstem** lesion, (2) **both hemispheres** affected (toxic, metabolic, infection, seizures), or (3) a big one-sided mass that **pushes** on the brainstem (herniation).
- **Toxic-metabolic** causes affect the whole brain **evenly** → **symmetric** exam, **reactive pupils** (usually), fluctuating, often asterixis/myoclonus.
- **Structural** causes are **asymmetric** → focal deficit, unequal pupils, gaze deviation.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

### The 5-minute exam

| Look at | Clue |
|---|---|
| **Level** | GCS or **FOUR score** (better in intubated). Responds to voice, pain, nothing? |
| **Pupils** | **Pinpoint** = opioids, pontine bleed · **One big, fixed** = CN III compression (**uncal herniation**) · **Both big** = anticholinergic, sympathomimetic, anoxia · Reactive pupils in deep coma = **metabolic** |
| **Eyes** | **Gaze deviation** (toward a destructive lesion, away from a seizure focus) · nystagmus · skew · doll's eyes |
| **Motor** | Lateralizing weakness · posturing (decorticate/decerebrate) · **asterixis** (metabolic) · **myoclonus** (uremia, anoxia, drugs) · **clonus/rigidity** (serotonin/NMS) |
| **Meninges / skin** | Neck stiffness, rash, track marks, cherry-red (CO), jaundice, bite marks (seizure) |
| **Breath** | Fruity (DKA), alcohol, uremic, fetor hepaticus; Kussmaul, Cheyne-Stokes |

:::tabs
=== Labs and tests (first round)
- **Glucose, BMP (Na, Ca, BUN/Cr), Mg, phos, LFTs, ammonia** (cirrhosis), CBC, **VBG/ABG** (CO₂), lactate, **TSH**, cortisol if shock.
- **UA**, cultures, CXR (infection in the elderly).
- **Tox:** acetaminophen, salicylate, ethanol, serum osm (osmolar gap), drug levels (lithium, valproate, digoxin, phenytoin), urine drug screen (limited), **carboxyhemoglobin**.
- **ECG** (QRS/QTc — TCA, sodium-channel blockers).
=== Second round
- **CT head** (non-contrast): focal signs, trauma/anticoagulants, no clear cause.
- **CTA** if basilar stroke suspected (fluctuating coma, eye-movement problems).
- **EEG:** fluctuating, twitching, eye deviation, unexplained → **non-convulsive status** in ~10–20% of ICU coma.
- **LP:** fever, immunosuppressed, no other explanation (after imaging if focal).
- **MRI:** Wernicke, encephalitis, PRES, small strokes, anoxic injury.
=== Delirium vs coma vs "locked-in"
- **Delirium:** acute, fluctuating, **inattention** (see Delirium chapter). Hypoactive delirium looks like sleepiness.
- **Coma:** eyes closed, no wake with stimulation.
- **Locked-in (pontine stroke):** awake, aware, cannot move except **vertical eye movements/blinking** — ask the patient to look up!
- **Psychogenic unresponsiveness:** eyes forced closed, arm-drop avoids face, nystagmus with cold calorics. Diagnosis of exclusion.
:::

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Glucose low?
Y: **D50 25–50 mL IV** (give **thiamine 100–500 mg IV** too if malnourished or alcohol use). Recheck in 15 min.
Q: Airway at risk (GCS ≤8, no gag, vomiting, hypercapnia not fixable)?
Y: Protect airway — call ICU / rapid response. BiPAP only if awake enough to protect the airway.
Q: Opioid toxidrome (pinpoint pupils, RR <12)?
Y: **Naloxone 0.04–0.4 mg IV**, repeat every 2–3 min; chronic opioid users → start low (avoid withdrawal/pulmonary edema).
Q: Focal signs, unequal pupils, anticoagulated, or trauma?
Y: **CT head now** (± CTA, stroke code if within window). Signs of herniation → **raise head, hyperventilate briefly, hypertonic saline/mannitol, neurosurgery** (see Raised ICP chapter).
Q: Fever or meningism?
Y: Cultures → **ceftriaxone + vancomycin + ampicillin (if >50) + acyclovir + dexamethasone** → LP.
Q: Still no cause?
Y: **EEG**; review drugs again; MRI; check ammonia, B12, thyroid, cortisol; consider toxicology consult.
:::

:::pearl Drugs that quietly cause encephalopathy (especially with AKI)
**Cefepime** (myoclonus, NCSE) · **gabapentin/pregabalin** · **morphine** (active metabolites) · **baclofen** · **acyclovir** · **lithium** · **valproate** (ammonia — even with normal LFTs) · **metronidazole** · **carbapenems** (seizures) · **ifosfamide** · **tramadol** · anticholinergics (diphenhydramine, oxybutynin, promethazine).
:::

:::dose
- **Dextrose:** D50 25–50 mL IV (or D10 250 mL).
- **Thiamine:** 500 mg IV q8h if Wernicke suspected (see Wernicke); 100 mg IV if just at risk.
- **Naloxone:** 0.04–0.4 mg IV q2–3 min (up to 2 mg); infusion at 2/3 of the effective bolus per hour if long-acting opioid.
- **Flumazenil:** generally **avoid** (seizures in chronic benzo users, TCA co-ingestion).
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Sending the patient to CT before checking **glucose**.
- Blaming "sundowning" or "sepsis" without an **exam** — miss the basilar stroke.
- **SpO₂ normal** on O₂ but **CO₂ 90** → check a gas.
- Big naloxone dose in a chronic opioid user → violent withdrawal, pulmonary edema.
- Missing **non-convulsive status** — subtle twitching of eyelids/fingers, eye deviation.
- **Valproate hyperammonemia** with normal LFTs.
- Forgetting **hypercalcemia**, **hyponatremia**, and **uremia**.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Comatose, pinpoint pupils, RR 6 | Opioid toxicity → naloxone |
| Comatose, pinpoint pupils, quadriplegia, hyperthermia, opioid screen negative | **Pontine hemorrhage** → CT |
| Coma with one fixed dilated pupil | Uncal herniation (CN III) → CT, ICP measures, neurosurgery |
| Awake, quadriplegic, mute, can blink and look up | **Locked-in syndrome** (ventral pons) — basilar occlusion |
| Fluctuating confusion, ICU, no seizures seen | EEG → non-convulsive status |
| CKD patient on cefepime, myoclonus, confusion | **Cefepime neurotoxicity** → stop/adjust, consider HD |
| Alcohol use disorder, hypoglycemic, confused | Thiamine + dextrose |
| On valproate, lethargic, normal LFTs | Check **ammonia** |
