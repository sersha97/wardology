:::bottom
- **On an anticoagulant + head injury (any)** → **CT head** (NICE: within 8 h even if well). New confusion, headache, vomiting or focal deficit → **now**.
- **Bleed on CT** → **reverse** the drug: warfarin → **4F-PCC + vitamin K 10 mg IV** · apixaban/rivaroxaban → **4F-PCC 50 U/kg** (andexanet **withdrawn** in US, Dec 2025) · dabigatran → **idarucizumab 5 g**. Hold antiplatelets; platelets only if surgery.
- **Neurosurgery** for all SDH; **SBP <160**; neuro checks q1–2h; repeat CT if worse.
- **Chronic SDH** = elderly, minor or no remembered fall, **weeks** of confusion, headache, gait change, hemiparesis — mimics dementia, delirium, TIA.
- **Steroids do not help** chronic SDH (Dex-CSDH: worse outcomes). **MMA embolization** reduces recurrence after surgery (EMBOLISE).
- Restart anticoagulation in a planned way — usually **after ~2–4 weeks** with neurosurgery agreement.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **Bridging veins** cross from the brain surface to the dural sinuses. With **brain atrophy** (age, alcohol), they stretch — a small jolt tears them → **venous** bleeding into the **subdural** space.
- Venous bleeding is **slow** → symptoms may appear **days to weeks** later, especially on anticoagulants.
- **Chronic SDH:** inflamed membranes grow **leaky new vessels** fed by the **middle meningeal artery (MMA)** → repeated small bleeds → the collection grows (why MMA embolization works).
- **Epidural hematoma** is different: **arterial** (MMA) after temporal bone fracture → rapid, lens-shaped, **lucid interval** then collapse.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

### Who needs a CT after a fall in the hospital

- **Anticoagulated** (warfarin, DOAC, therapeutic heparin) — **all**, even if well.
- Any **loss of consciousness**, amnesia, new confusion, vomiting, severe headache, seizure, focal deficit, GCS <15.
- Age **≥65** with any head injury (Canadian CT Head Rule), **dangerous mechanism** (stairs, height).
- **Antiplatelets alone** + head strike: low threshold, especially if elderly.
- Cannot assess (dementia, intoxicated) → scan.

### Labs

- **INR, aPTT**, CBC/platelets, fibrinogen, creatinine (DOAC clearance), **time of last DOAC dose**, anti-Xa level if available.

:::tabs
=== Delayed bleeding
- Delayed SDH after a **normal first CT** on anticoagulants is **uncommon (~1%)** and rarely needs surgery.
- **No routine repeat CT** for everyone — repeat if **any** new symptom, supratherapeutic INR, or high-risk features. Observe and educate.
=== Chronic SDH work-up
- CT: **hypodense** crescent, may be **bilateral**, "mixed density" (acute-on-chronic).
- Isodense subacute SDH is easy to miss: look for **effaced sulci and midline shift**. MRI if unsure.
- Check: coags, platelets, alcohol use, falls cause (syncope, orthostasis, meds — **ask why they fell**).
:::

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: SDH on CT and on an anticoagulant?
Y: **Reverse now** (see dose box). **Stop** anticoagulant and antiplatelets. **Neurosurgery consult.**
Q: Large (>10 mm thick or >5 mm midline shift), symptomatic, or worsening?
Y: **Surgical evacuation** (craniotomy for acute; burr holes/twist drill for chronic) ± **MMA embolization**.
N: Small, stable → **observe**: neuro checks, **repeat CT at ~6–24 h**, SBP <160, seizure only if seizures occur.
Q: Chronic SDH, mild symptoms?
Y: Observation ± **MMA embolization** (standalone embolization trials ongoing). **No dexamethasone.**
Q: Patient fell — why?
Y: Work up **syncope**, orthostasis, arrhythmia, sedating drugs, vision, gait, **delirium**. PT/OT, home safety.
:::

:::tabs
=== Restarting anticoagulation
- Weigh **thrombosis risk** (mechanical valve, recent VTE, AF with high CHA₂DS₂-VASc) vs **re-bleed risk**.
- Typical timing after SDH: **~2–4 weeks**, after stable imaging and neurosurgery agreement. Earlier (days) for very high thrombotic risk (mechanical mitral valve).
- **VTE prophylaxis** (SC heparin) usually OK **24–48 h** after a stable CT.
- Prefer a **DOAC** over warfarin when restarting (lower intracranial bleeding).
- AF with recurrent bleeds → consider **left atrial appendage occlusion**.
- **Falls alone are not a reason** to stop anticoagulation in AF — a patient would need to fall ~300 times a year for the bleeding risk to outweigh the stroke benefit.
=== Seizures
- Seizures in acute SDH → treat (levetiracetam).
- Prophylaxis: short course (≤7 days) sometimes used by neurosurgery; no long-term prophylaxis without seizures.
:::

:::dose
- **Warfarin:** **4F-PCC** 25–50 U/kg by INR (or fixed 1,500–2,000 U per protocol) + **vitamin K 10 mg IV**. Recheck INR 15–30 min.
- **Apixaban / rivaroxaban / edoxaban:** **4F-PCC 50 U/kg** (max ~5,000 U) or fixed 2,000 U per protocol. Andexanet no longer available in the US.
- **Dabigatran:** **idarucizumab 5 g IV** (2 × 2.5 g); dialysis if unavailable.
- **Heparin (UFH):** protamine 1 mg per 100 U given in last 2–3 h (max 50 mg). **Enoxaparin:** protamine 1 mg per 1 mg if within 8 h (partial).
- **Platelet transfusion for antiplatelet ICH:** not without surgery (PATCH — harm).
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Sending the well-looking anticoagulated patient back to bed **without a CT** after a fall.
- Missing an **isodense** subacute SDH.
- Blaming new confusion in an elderly patient on dementia or UTI without imaging after a fall.
- Using **dexamethasone** for chronic SDH.
- Platelet transfusion for patients on aspirin/clopidogrel who are not going to surgery.
- Never restarting anticoagulation in AF "because she falls."
- Forgetting to look for **why** the patient fell.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| 82-year-old on apixaban fell, hit head, feels fine | **CT head** |
| Same, CT: 6 mm SDH, no shift | Reverse with **4F-PCC**, neurosurgery, repeat CT, neuro checks |
| Alcohol use disorder, 3 weeks of confusion and gait change, no clear trauma | **Chronic SDH** → CT |
| Head trauma, brief LOC, lucid, then sudden coma; lens-shaped bleed | **Epidural hematoma** (MMA) → emergent evacuation |
| Crescent across suture lines | Subdural |
| Chronic SDH — give dexamethasone? | **No** (worse outcomes) |
| What reduces recurrence after chronic SDH evacuation? | **MMA embolization** |
| Warfarin, INR 4.5, SDH | 4F-PCC + IV vitamin K 10 mg |
| Dabigatran with ICH | Idarucizumab |
