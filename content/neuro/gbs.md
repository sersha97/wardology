:::bottom
- **Weak legs going up + weak or absent reflexes** (days to 4 weeks) = GBS until proven otherwise. Often after diarrhea (*Campylobacter*) or a cold.
- **Breathing is the killer, not the weakness.** Check **FVC and NIF at the bedside now** and every 4–6 h. **20-30-40 rule** → ICU.
- **Swallow screen.** Weak bulbar muscles = aspiration. NPO until safe.
- Can't walk unaided (or getting worse fast) → **IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day × 5 days** or **plasma exchange (PLEX) × 5**. Equal benefit; **not both**; **steroids do not help**.
- Monitor **heart rate, BP and rhythm** (telemetry): autonomic swings can kill.
- Start **DVT prophylaxis**, **pain** control (gabapentinoid/TCA), bowel and bladder care.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- An infection teaches the immune system to make **antibodies** that look like the bug. The same antibodies **cross-react** with peripheral nerve (molecular mimicry).
- Two main forms: **AIDP** (attacks **myelin**, the insulation — most common in North America/Europe) and **AMAN/AMSAN** (attacks the **axon** — after *Campylobacter*, slower recovery).
- Peripheral nerve = **lower motor neuron** → **flaccid, areflexic** weakness, no Babinski, no sensory level.
- Triggers: *Campylobacter jejuni* (~30%), CMV, EBV, Zika, influenza, *Mycoplasma*, recent surgery; rarely vaccines.
- Course: worse for up to **4 weeks** (nadir usually ~2 weeks) → plateau → slow recovery over weeks–months.

:::analogy
Nerves are electric cables. In GBS the immune system **strips the plastic insulation** off. The signal leaks and slows down. The longest cables (to the feet) fail first, then the shorter ones (hands, face, diaphragm). Insulation grows back — so most people recover — but it takes months.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

### Clinical picture

- **Bilateral, fairly symmetric** weakness, usually **legs first, ascending**. Can start in arms or face.
- **Reflexes reduced or absent** in weak limbs (can be normal early — ~10%).
- **Pain** (back, legs) in up to two-thirds — often the first symptom. Do not call it "just sciatica."
- Mild **tingling** of hands/feet; objective sensory loss is often small.
- **Cranial nerves:** bilateral facial weakness, dysphagia, diplopia.
- **Autonomic:** tachycardia, brady-pauses, labile BP, ileus, urinary retention.

### Tests

| Test | What you expect | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **LP** | **Albuminocytologic dissociation**: protein ↑, cells **<50** (usually <5) | Normal protein in week 1 does **not** exclude GBS (~30–50%) |
| **NCS/EMG** | Demyelinating (slow conduction, blocks) or axonal | Confirms subtype; may be normal very early |
| **FVC / NIF** | Baseline and trend | Predicts intubation |
| **ECG / telemetry** | Arrhythmias | Autonomic dysfunction |
| **Labs** | BMP, Mg, phos, K, CK, glucose, HIV, CBC | Rule out mimics |
| **MRI spine** (if any doubt) | Root enhancement; **no cord lesion** | Excludes cord compression/myelitis |

:::warn Red flags — think "not GBS"
CSF cells **>50** (HIV, Lyme, lymphoma, polio/West Nile) · **sensory level** or early bladder failure (cord) · marked **asymmetry** · fever at onset · progression **>8 weeks** (CIDP).
:::

### Variants (boards love these)

- **Miller Fisher:** **ophthalmoplegia + ataxia + areflexia**. **Anti-GQ1b** antibodies.
- **Bickerstaff encephalitis:** Miller Fisher + drowsiness/hyperreflexia (anti-GQ1b).
- **Pharyngeal-cervical-brachial:** neck, throat and arm weakness.
- **AMAN:** pure motor, axonal, *Campylobacter*, anti-GM1.

### Mimics

Spinal cord compression or myelitis · **tick paralysis** (search the scalp!) · **botulism** (descending, pupils, no sensory) · myasthenia (fatigable, normal reflexes) · hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia, hypermagnesemia · critical illness neuropathy · West Nile/polio (fever, CSF cells) · porphyria · heavy metals.

## 💊 Management

{{fig:gbs-203040}}

:::flow
Q: FVC <20 mL/kg, NIF weaker than −30, MEP <40, or >30% fall; or cannot swallow/cough?
Y: **ICU now.** Elective intubation is safer than a crash intubation. Avoid **succinylcholine** (hyperkalemia).
N: Admit to a monitored bed. FVC/NIF every 4–6 h. Telemetry.
Q: Cannot walk 10 m unaided, or getting worse fast, within ~2–4 weeks of onset?
Y: **IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day × 5 days** (total 2 g/kg) **or PLEX** 5 exchanges over ~2 weeks. Pick by availability and patient factors.
N: Mild and stable (walks unaided): supportive care; treat if it gets worse.
Q: Worse again after initial improvement (treatment-related fluctuation)?
Y: Often a second course (discuss with neurology). If still progressing **>8 weeks** → think **CIDP**.
:::

:::tabs
=== IVIG or PLEX?
| | IVIG | PLEX |
|---|---|---|
| Best when | Easy to give, most hospitals | Center with apheresis |
| Avoid when | IgA deficiency (anaphylaxis), renal failure, thrombosis risk, volume overload | Hemodynamic instability, sepsis, no central line, bleeding |
| Side effects | Headache, aseptic meningitis, **AKI** (sucrose products), **thrombosis**, hemolysis | Hypotension, line infection, hypocalcemia (citrate), coagulopathy |

**Never sequential** (PLEX washes out IVIG). **No corticosteroids** — no benefit, may slow recovery.
=== Supportive care
- **Lungs:** FVC/NIF schedule; incentive spirometry; HOB up; suction; early ICU.
- **Swallow:** SLP evaluation; NPO or tube feeds if bulbar.
- **Autonomic:** telemetry; treat BP spikes with **short-acting** agents only (crash can follow); bradycardia → atropine, rarely pacer.
- **Pain:** gabapentin/pregabalin or TCA; opioids sparingly (ileus).
- **DVT:** enoxaparin until walking.
- **Bowel/bladder:** bowel regimen, bladder scans.
- **Skin, eyes** (taped/lubricated if facial weakness), early **PT/OT**.
- **Mood:** patients are often fully awake and scared. Explain; consider SSRI.
=== Prognosis
- Most walk again; **~20% cannot walk at 6 months**, ~5% die (respiratory, autonomic, PE, infection).
- **Worse prognosis:** age >60, preceding diarrhea (*Campylobacter*), rapid onset, severe weakness at nadir, axonal NCS.
- **EGRIS** score (days from onset, facial/bulbar weakness, MRC sum) predicts need for ventilation.
:::

:::dose
- **IVIG:** 0.4 g/kg/day IV × 5 days (use ideal or adjusted body weight in obesity per pharmacy). Pre-hydrate; check creatinine; slow the rate if headache.
- **PLEX:** 5 exchanges (~200–250 mL/kg total) over ~2 weeks.
- **Pain:** gabapentin 100–300 mg TID, titrate (renal dose).
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Trusting **SpO₂ or ABG** to judge breathing. They are normal until the end. **Measure FVC/NIF.**
- Using a poor seal: a weak face can give falsely low FVC — use a mask/mouthpiece and coach.
- Labeling early back/leg pain + weakness as "sciatica" and sending the patient home.
- Normal CSF protein in week 1 → "not GBS." Protein rises later.
- Treating BP spikes with long-acting drugs → severe hypotension after the swing.
- Giving steroids. Forgetting DVT prophylaxis.
- Missing **tick paralysis** — remove the tick and the patient recovers in hours.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Diarrhea 2 weeks ago, now ascending weakness, absent reflexes | GBS (*Campylobacter*) → FVC/NIF, IVIG or PLEX |
| CSF: protein 120, WBC 2 | Albuminocytologic dissociation |
| CSF: protein 120, WBC 80 | Not typical GBS → HIV, Lyme, lymphoma, West Nile |
| GBS patient, best test to decide ICU | **FVC / NIF** (not ABG) |
| Ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, areflexia | Miller Fisher — anti-GQ1b |
| GBS, which treatment is NOT useful? | **Corticosteroids** |
| Descending paralysis, dilated pupils, constipation, honey/home canning | **Botulism** → antitoxin |
| Ascending paralysis, child, recent hike | **Tick paralysis** → find and remove tick |
| Weakness progressing >8 weeks, relapsing | **CIDP** → steroids work here |
