:::bottom
- New or worse neuro deficit in a known MS patient: **first look for infection, fever or heat** (UA, CBC, CXR). That is a **pseudo-relapse** — treat the trigger, **no steroids**.
- **True relapse** = new or worse symptom **≥24 h**, no fever, **≥30 days** after the last attack.
- **Disabling relapse** (vision, weakness, ataxia, bladder) → **methylprednisolone 1 g IV/PO daily × 3–5 days**. High-dose oral is as good as IV.
- No response after steroids and still severe → **plasma exchange (PLEX)**.
- **Severe optic neuritis, long cord lesion (≥3 segments), area postrema (intractable hiccups/vomiting)** → send **AQP4-IgG** and **MOG-IgG**. NMOSD is treated differently — some MS drugs make it **worse**.
- Steroids speed recovery; they do **not** change long-term disability.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **MS:** T and B cells cross into the brain and spinal cord and strip **myelin** (CNS insulation) in patches called **plaques**. Axons also get damaged → disability over time.
- Typical lesions: **periventricular** (Dawson fingers), **juxtacortical**, **infratentorial**, **spinal cord**, and **optic nerve**.
- Diagnosis rule: damage spread in **space** (different places) and in **time** (different ages). The **2024 McDonald** revision adds the **optic nerve** as a 5th site and lets **central vein sign**, **paramagnetic rim lesions** and **CSF kappa free light chains** support the diagnosis.
- **NMOSD** = antibodies against **aquaporin-4** (water channel on astrocytes). Attacks are **severe** and damage is permanent → prevent every attack.
- **MOGAD** = antibodies against MOG (myelin protein). Often after infection; children and young adults; ADEM-like.

:::analogy
Old MS lesions are like **scars on old wiring**. Heat, fever or infection makes the wiring work even worse for a while — the lights flicker (pseudo-relapse, **Uhthoff**). Cool down and treat the fever and the lights come back. A **true relapse** is a **new** cut in the wire.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

{{fig:relapse-check}}

### Classic syndromes

| Syndrome | Clues |
|---|---|
| **Optic neuritis** | Painful eye movement, central vision loss, washed-out **red color**, **RAPD**; disc often normal (retrobulbar) |
| **Partial myelitis** | Numbness ascending from feet, band-like trunk feeling, **Lhermitte** (electric shock down spine on neck flexion) |
| **Brainstem** | **Internuclear ophthalmoplegia (INO)** — especially **bilateral** in a young adult = MS until proven otherwise |
| **Cerebellar** | Ataxia, intention tremor, scanning speech |

### Work-up of a new attack

- **MRI brain + spine with gadolinium**: new or enhancing lesions; length of cord lesion.
- **Rule out infection:** UA/urine culture, CBC, CXR if symptoms. **UTI is the #1 pseudo-relapse trigger.**
- First attack / atypical: **AQP4-IgG** (cell-based assay), **MOG-IgG**, LP (oligoclonal bands, kappa FLC index, cell count), B12, HIV, syphilis, ANA/SSA, ACE if sarcoid suspected.
- On **natalizumab** (or other immunotherapy) with slowly progressive cortical symptoms → think **PML**: MRI + CSF **JC virus PCR**.

:::tabs
=== MS vs NMOSD vs MOGAD
| | MS | NMOSD (AQP4+) | MOGAD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | Women 20–40 | Women, older, Black/Asian; other autoimmunity | Children, young adults, after infection |
| Optic neuritis | Unilateral, mild–moderate | **Severe, bilateral**, chiasm | **Bilateral**, swollen disc, good recovery |
| Cord | **Short** (<2 segments), peripheral | **Long (LETM ≥3 segments)**, central | Long, conus, gray matter ("H sign") |
| Brain | Periventricular, Dawson fingers | Area postrema (**hiccups, vomiting**), hypothalamus | ADEM-like, fluffy |
| CSF | **Oligoclonal bands** (~90%) | Neutrophils, OCB often absent | OCB usually absent |
| Long-term | DMT | **Eculizumab/ravulizumab, inebilizumab, satralizumab, rituximab** | Often monophasic; treat if relapses |
| Danger | — | **Interferon, natalizumab, fingolimod can worsen** | — |
=== Transverse myelitis
- **Myelopathy over hours–days**: bilateral weakness/numbness, **sensory level**, bladder.
- **First: rule out compression** — **emergency MRI spine** (see Cauda equina chapter).
- Causes: MS, **NMOSD**, MOGAD, post-infectious, lupus/Sjögren, sarcoid, **B12/copper deficiency** (subacute), HIV, syphilis, VZV/HSV, spinal dural AV fistula, cord infarct (sudden, anterior spinal artery).
- Treat: **IV methylprednisolone** 1 g × 5 days ± early **PLEX** if severe.
:::

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Fever, infection or heat exposure?
Y: **Pseudo-relapse.** Treat the infection, cool, rest. Symptoms fade in 24–48 h. No steroids.
N: Check: new symptom ≥24 h, ≥30 days from last attack?
Q: Disabling (vision, motor, ataxia, bladder, brainstem)?
Y: **Methylprednisolone 1 g daily × 3–5 days** (IV, or 1,250 mg oral prednisone equivalent). Check glucose, BP, mood, sleep; PPI if GI risk.
N: Mild sensory relapse → often observe; outpatient neurology.
Q: Severe attack not improving after steroids (or AQP4/MOG+)?
Y: **PLEX** 5–7 exchanges. Start early in NMOSD.
Q: Frequent relapses or new MRI activity?
Y: Neurology to start or switch a **disease-modifying therapy**.
:::

:::dose
- **Methylprednisolone:** 1 g IV daily × 3–5 days. Oral bioequivalent: prednisone 1,250 mg/day (25 × 50 mg tablets) — as effective (COPOUSEP). Taper usually not needed.
- **PLEX:** 5–7 exchanges every other day.
- Symptomatic: spasticity → baclofen/tizanidine; neuropathic pain → gabapentin; bladder → check PVR before anticholinergics.
:::

:::pearl Hospitalist checklist for the MS patient on a DMT
- **Natalizumab** → PML risk (JCV). **Anti-CD20** (ocrelizumab, ofatumumab, ublituximab), **fingolimod/siponimod/ozanimod**, cladribine, alemtuzumab → infections, low IgG, **poor vaccine response**, herpes reactivation.
- **Stopping fingolimod or natalizumab abruptly** → severe **rebound** relapse. Ask neurology before holding.
- **Fingolimod/S1P drugs:** bradycardia/AV block on first dose; macular edema.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Steroids for a **UTI-triggered** pseudo-relapse.
- Missing **cord compression** in "transverse myelitis" — image first.
- Treating NMOSD as MS (interferon, natalizumab) → disastrous relapses. Send **AQP4-IgG** in severe or long-cord attacks.
- Hiccups and vomiting for days → "GI problem." Think **area postrema** (NMOSD).
- New MS diagnosis based on non-specific white-matter spots (migraine, small-vessel disease). MS needs the **right clinical story**.
- Forgetting glucose monitoring and insomnia/psychosis risk with 1 g methylprednisolone.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Young woman, painful eye movement, central scotoma, RAPD | Optic neuritis → MRI brain (MS risk) ± IV steroids |
| Bilateral INO in a 25-year-old | MS |
| MS patient, symptoms worse in hot shower, resolve when cool | Uhthoff phenomenon (pseudo-relapse) |
| MS patient, new leg weakness, fever, positive UA | Pseudo-relapse → treat UTI, no steroids |
| Longitudinally extensive myelitis + severe optic neuritis | **NMOSD** → AQP4-IgG |
| Intractable hiccups and vomiting, then myelitis | Area postrema syndrome — NMOSD |
| Natalizumab, progressive hemiparesis and aphasia, no enhancement | **PML** → CSF JC virus PCR |
| CSF oligoclonal bands | Support MS (dissemination in time in 2017/2024 criteria) |
| Disabling relapse, oral vs IV steroids? | Equivalent at high dose |
