:::bottom
- **Fatigable** weakness: droopy lids and double vision that get **worse at the end of the day**, plus bulbar (chewing, swallowing, speech), neck and proximal limb weakness. **Reflexes and sensation are normal.**
- **Crisis** = weakness that needs (or nearly needs) a **ventilator** or **feeding tube**. Watch for: weak neck flexion, nasal voice, choking, **can't count to 20 in one breath**.
- Check **FVC and NIF** now and every 2–6 h. Use the **20-30-40 rule**. Low threshold for **ICU** and **elective intubation** (BiPAP is OK early if no bulbar failure).
- Crisis treatment: **IVIG 2 g/kg over 2–5 days** or **PLEX** (5 exchanges). Start or continue steroids carefully.
- **Find the trigger:** infection, surgery, a **new drug**, steroid start, tapering.
- In crisis on the ventilator → **hold pyridostigmine** (secretions).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- The nerve releases **acetylcholine (ACh)**. ACh binds to **receptors (AChR)** on muscle → contraction.
- In MG, **antibodies** block and destroy the AChR (~85% of generalized MG), or attack **MuSK** (~5–8%) or **LRP4**.
- Each repeat contraction releases a little less ACh. With fewer receptors, the safety margin runs out → **fatigue with repetition**.
- Small, busy muscles fail first: **eyelids and eye muscles** (ocular MG; ~50% start here), then bulbar, neck, limbs, **diaphragm**.
- **Thymus** plays a role: **thymoma** in ~10–15% (CT chest for everyone); thymic hyperplasia in young patients.

:::analogy
The nerve is a mail carrier, ACh the letters, the receptors are mailboxes. In MG, the antibodies **glue the mailboxes shut**. The first few letters get in. By the tenth delivery the carrier is tired, sends fewer letters, and none find an open box — so the muscle stops listening.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

### Bedside

- **Ptosis** that worsens with **sustained upgaze** (30–60 s). **Ice-pack test**: 2 min of ice on the lid improves ptosis (cold slows the enzyme that breaks down ACh).
- **Neck flexion** weakness tracks with diaphragm weakness.
- **Single-breath count** <20 → worry. Nasal voice, regurgitation, wet cough → bulbar.
- Normal **pupils**, **reflexes** and **sensation** (if not → look elsewhere).

### Tests

| Test | Notes |
|---|---|
| **AChR antibodies** | Positive in ~85% generalized, ~50% ocular |
| **MuSK** antibodies (if AChR negative) | Women, bulbar/neck, **poor response to pyridostigmine**, great response to PLEX/rituximab |
| **Repetitive nerve stimulation** | **Decrement** >10% |
| **Single-fiber EMG** | Most sensitive (jitter); not specific |
| **CT chest** | Look for **thymoma** |
| TSH | Autoimmune thyroid disease is common together |
| Bedside FVC / NIF | Every patient with generalized or bulbar symptoms |

:::tabs
=== MG vs LEMS vs botulism
| | Myasthenia gravis | Lambert-Eaton (LEMS) | Botulism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target | Post-synaptic **AChR** | Pre-synaptic **Ca²⁺ channel** (VGCC) | Pre-synaptic ACh release (toxin) |
| Pattern | Eyes/bulbar first, **worse with use** | **Legs** first, **improves with use** | **Descending**, eyes/bulbar first |
| Reflexes | Normal | **Reduced, improve after exercise** | Reduced |
| Autonomic | No | **Dry mouth**, constipation, ED | **Dilated pupils**, ileus |
| Associated | Thymoma | **Small cell lung cancer** (~50%) | Home canning, wounds (IVDU), infant honey |
| Treatment | Pyridostigmine, immunotherapy | Treat cancer, **amifampridine** | **Antitoxin**, ICU |
=== Myasthenic vs cholinergic crisis
- **Myasthenic crisis** (common): not enough ACh effect → weakness, dry, normal pupils.
- **Cholinergic crisis** (rare, too much pyridostigmine, usually >120 mg q3h): weakness **plus** SLUDGE — salivation, sweating, diarrhea, cramps, **small pupils**, bradycardia, fasciculations.
- In practice: **treat as myasthenic crisis**, hold pyridostigmine, protect the airway. The edrophonium (Tensilon) test is no longer used.
:::

## 💊 Management

{{fig:mg-drugs}}

:::flow
Q: Bulbar failure, FVC <20 mL/kg, NIF weaker than −30, or rising PaCO₂?
Y: **ICU.** Intubate early (or **BiPAP** if no bulbar failure and not hypercapnic — can avoid intubation). Hold pyridostigmine. Rocuronium at a **reduced dose** + sugammadex; avoid succinylcholine.
N: Monitored bed, FVC/NIF every 4–6 h, swallow eval, aspiration precautions.
Q: Crisis or impending crisis?
Y: **IVIG 2 g/kg** (over 2–5 days) **or PLEX** 5 exchanges. PLEX works a little faster and is preferred for **MuSK** and severe crisis.
Q: On steroids? Not yet?
Y: Start **prednisone** with/after IVIG/PLEX (high dose can worsen weakness in the first 1–2 weeks — start in a monitored setting, or start low and go up). Add a steroid-sparing agent.
Q: Trigger found (infection, drug, surgery)?
Y: Treat the infection with **MG-safe antibiotics** (beta-lactams, doxycycline). Stop the culprit drug.
:::

:::tabs
=== Chronic therapy (what the patient is on)
| Drug | Role |
|---|---|
| **Pyridostigmine** | Symptom relief only (does not change the disease). Side effects: GI cramps, diarrhea, sweat, secretions |
| **Prednisone** | Most patients; slow taper |
| Azathioprine, mycophenolate | Steroid-sparing (take months to work) |
| **Rituximab** | MuSK-positive MG (very effective) |
| **Complement inhibitors** — eculizumab, ravulizumab, **zilucoplan** (SC) | AChR+ generalized MG. **Meningococcal vaccine** required (risk of *Neisseria*) |
| **FcRn blockers** — efgartigimod, rozanolixizumab, **nipocalimab** (2025) | Lower IgG quickly; AChR+ (nipocalimab and rozanolixizumab also MuSK+) |
| **Inebilizumab** (anti-CD19, approved Dec 2025) | AChR+ or MuSK+ generalized MG, twice-yearly dosing |
| **Thymectomy** | Thymoma (all); AChR+ generalized MG age ~18–65 (MGTX trial) |
=== Peri-operative / admitted MG patient
- Continue **pyridostigmine and immunotherapy** on schedule; IV pyridostigmine = **1/30** of oral dose if NPO.
- Stress-dose steroids if on chronic prednisone.
- Tell anesthesia: reduced dose of non-depolarizing blockers, sugammadex for reversal.
- Check **every new order** against the "drugs that worsen MG" list.
- Post-op: FVC/NIF checks; early mobilization; pain control without heavy sedation.
- Pregnancy: MG may flare post-partum; **magnesium sulfate is contraindicated** (use levetiracetam for eclampsia seizures).
:::

:::dose
- **IVIG:** 2 g/kg total over 2–5 days (e.g., 0.4 g/kg/day × 5). Check creatinine; IgA level if time allows.
- **PLEX:** 5 exchanges every other day.
- **Pyridostigmine:** 30–60 mg PO q4–6h (max ~120 mg q4h). Hold if intubated with heavy secretions.
- **Prednisone:** start 10–20 mg/day and increase by 5–10 mg every few days, or high dose (~1 mg/kg) with IVIG/PLEX coverage in a monitored bed.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Trusting **SpO₂**. Hypoxemia is a **late** sign; hypercapnia comes before.
- Giving **IV magnesium**, a fluoroquinolone, aminoglycoside or macrolide to a weak MG patient without a plan.
- Starting **high-dose steroids** on the floor without monitoring → early worsening.
- "Ocular only" — up to half of ocular MG generalizes within 2 years.
- Forgetting **meningococcal vaccination** (or prophylaxis) for complement inhibitors.
- Using **succinylcholine** (unpredictable) or normal doses of rocuronium (prolonged block).
- Missing **checkpoint-inhibitor MG**: check **troponin and CK** (MG + myositis + myocarditis overlap).

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Ptosis and diplopia worse in the evening, normal pupils | MG → AChR antibodies |
| MG patient with pneumonia gets levofloxacin, now dysphagia | Drug-triggered crisis → FVC/NIF, IVIG or PLEX |
| Best test to follow respiratory status in MG | **FVC / NIF** |
| New MG diagnosis, next imaging | **CT chest** (thymoma) |
| Smoker, leg weakness improves with repeated effort, dry mouth | **LEMS** → CT chest (small cell) |
| MG crisis, AChR-negative, MuSK-positive | **PLEX** (and rituximab long-term); poor pyridostigmine response |
| MG patient, severe pre-eclampsia | **Avoid magnesium**; use levetiracetam |
| Excess pyridostigmine: weakness + miosis + diarrhea + bradycardia | **Cholinergic crisis** |
| Starting eculizumab | **Meningococcal vaccine** first |
