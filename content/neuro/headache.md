:::bottom
- First question: **primary** (migraine, tension, cluster) or **secondary** (something dangerous)? Use **SNOOP** red flags.
- **Thunderclap** → see SAH chapter. **Fever + headache** → meningitis/encephalitis. **New headache age >50** → **ESR/CRP** for **GCA**.
- **Migraine on the ward:** IV fluids + **prochlorperazine 10 mg IV** or **metoclopramide 10 mg IV** (+ diphenhydramine for akathisia) ± **ketorolac**. Add **dexamethasone 10 mg** to prevent bounce-back. **Avoid opioids and butalbital.**
- **Suspected GCA** with visual symptoms → **steroids now**, biopsy or ultrasound within 1–2 weeks.
- Headache + **papilledema** → think **IIH** or **cerebral venous thrombosis (CVT)** → MRI + **MR venogram**.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- The brain itself feels no pain. Pain comes from **meninges, blood vessels, venous sinuses, nerves and scalp muscles**.
- **Migraine:** brain hyperexcitability → **trigeminovascular** activation → **CGRP** release → vessel dilation and sterile inflammation → throbbing pain, light/sound sensitivity, nausea. Aura = slowly spreading wave of cortical depression.
- **Secondary headaches** stretch or inflame these pain structures: blood (SAH), pressure (tumor, IIH, CVT), inflammation (meningitis, GCA), low pressure (CSF leak).

## 🔎 Diagnosis

{{fig:snoop}}

### Exam you must do

- **Fundi** (papilledema, retinal hemorrhage) — a phone camera or non-mydriatic photo helps.
- Visual acuity/fields, pupils, eye movements (CN VI palsy = raised ICP), neck stiffness, temporal arteries (tender, no pulse), BP.
- Full neuro exam: drift, gait, reflexes.

### Who needs imaging

| Picture | Test |
|---|---|
| Thunderclap, new focal sign, AMS, papilledema, trauma on anticoagulants | **CT head** now |
| Suspected **CVT** | **CT/MR venogram** |
| Suspected **IIH** | **MRI + MRV** first, then **LP** with opening pressure |
| Suspected **dissection** | **CTA head and neck** |
| Typical migraine, normal exam, same as usual | **No imaging** |

:::tabs
=== Must-not-miss list
| Condition | Key clue | First step |
|---|---|---|
| **SAH** | Thunderclap | CT (see SAH chapter) |
| **Meningitis/encephalitis** | Fever, AMS | Cultures, abx/acyclovir, LP |
| **GCA** | Age >50, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, visual loss, PMR | ESR/CRP, **prednisone now** if suspected |
| **CVT** | Pregnancy/post-partum, OCP, cancer, dehydration; seizure, papilledema | MR/CT venogram → **anticoagulate** (even with hemorrhage) |
| **IIH** | Young woman, obesity, **papilledema**, pulsatile tinnitus, transient visual obscurations | MRI/MRV normal → LP OP **>25 cmH₂O** |
| **Hypertensive emergency / PRES** | Very high BP, visual changes, seizures | Lower BP (see HTN emergency) |
| **Acute angle-closure glaucoma** | Red painful eye, halos, fixed mid-dilated pupil | Ophthalmology, pressure-lowering drops |
| **CO poisoning** | Several household members, winter, heaters | **Carboxyhemoglobin** (co-oximetry) |
| **Pituitary apoplexy** | Sudden, visual loss, ophthalmoplegia | MRI, steroids |
| **Subdural** | Elderly, anticoagulated, fall | CT |
=== Primary headaches
| | Migraine | Tension-type | Cluster |
|---|---|---|---|
| Location | Usually one side | Both sides, band | **One side, around the eye** |
| Quality | Throbbing | Pressing | Boring, stabbing, **excruciating** |
| Length | 4–72 h | 30 min – 7 days | **15–180 min**, up to 8/day, same time daily |
| Features | Nausea, light/sound sensitivity, aura | Few | **Tearing, red eye, runny nose, ptosis/miosis**, restless (paces) |
| Acute Rx | NSAID, triptan, gepant; ED cocktail | NSAID, acetaminophen | **100% O₂ 12–15 L/min** NRB × 15 min, **SC sumatriptan** |
| Prevention | Propranolol, topiramate, amitriptyline, CGRP mAb | Amitriptyline | **Verapamil** (ECG); prednisone bridge |
=== Medication overuse headache
- Daily headache + simple analgesics ≥15 days/month or triptans/opioids/combination ≥10 days/month for >3 months.
- Fix: **stop the overused drug** (taper opioids/butalbital), bridge (NSAID course, steroid burst, nerve block), start **prevention**.
- **Butalbital and opioids** are the worst offenders — avoid starting them in the hospital.
:::

## 💊 Management

### The ward migraine "cocktail"

:::flow
Q: Red flags absent, typical migraine?
Y: **IV fluids 1 L** + **prochlorperazine 10 mg IV** (or **metoclopramide 10–20 mg IV**) + **diphenhydramine 25 mg IV** (akathisia prevention).
Q: Still pain?
Y: Add **ketorolac 15–30 mg IV** (if renal function and bleeding risk OK) ± **magnesium 1–2 g IV** (aura). Consider **valproate 500–1000 mg IV** or a **greater occipital nerve block**.
Q: Pain >72 h (status migrainosus)?
Y: **DHE 0.5–1 mg IV** (after antiemetic; not with triptan in 24 h, not in CAD/pregnancy) repeated q8h; neurology.
Q: Going home?
Y: **Dexamethasone 10 mg IV/PO once** (fewer return visits). Plan: triptan/gepant, limit analgesic days, prevention if ≥4 days/month.
:::

:::tabs
=== GCA quick plan
- **Suspect:** age >50, new headache, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, PMR, fever/anemia, **ESR/CRP high**.
- **Visual symptoms** (amaurosis, diplopia) → **methylprednisolone 500–1000 mg IV × 3 days** then prednisone 1 mg/kg. No visual symptoms → **prednisone 40–60 mg/day**.
- **Do not delay steroids** for biopsy. Biopsy (or temporal artery **ultrasound**, "halo sign") within 1–2 weeks is still positive.
- Add **tocilizumab** for steroid-sparing (rheumatology); bone protection; PJP prophylaxis consideration.
=== CVT quick plan
- Headache (90%) ± seizure, focal deficit, papilledema, AMS. Risk: pregnancy/post-partum, **estrogen**, thrombophilia, cancer, dehydration, infection (mastoiditis).
- **Anticoagulate** with **LMWH/heparin** — **even if there is hemorrhage** on CT (venous infarct).
- Long term: warfarin or **DOAC** (dabigatran studied; AHA 2024 accepts DOACs) for 3–12 months.
- Treat ICP and seizures; worsening → endovascular therapy/neurosurgery.
=== IIH quick plan
- **Weight loss** (5–10%; GLP-1 RA helpful) + **acetazolamide** 500 mg BID → up to 2–4 g/day.
- **Visual field testing** — vision loss is the danger. Rapid vision loss → **urgent CSF shunt / venous sinus stent / optic nerve sheath fenestration**.
- Look for drugs: **tetracyclines**, vitamin A/retinoids, **lithium**, steroid withdrawal.
=== Post-dural puncture headache
- After LP/epidural; **worse upright, better lying** within minutes.
- Fluids, caffeine, analgesics; **epidural blood patch** if severe or >24–48 h.
:::

:::dose
- **Prochlorperazine:** 10 mg IV · **Metoclopramide:** 10–20 mg IV · **Diphenhydramine:** 25 mg IV.
- **Ketorolac:** 15–30 mg IV once (avoid if CrCl low, bleeding, peptic ulcer).
- **Dexamethasone:** 10 mg IV/PO once.
- **Sumatriptan:** 6 mg SC (avoid in CAD, uncontrolled HTN, stroke, pregnancy relative).
- **DHE:** 0.5–1 mg IV q8h (max 3 mg/day); pre-treat with antiemetic.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- **Opioids** for migraine → rebound, return visits, medication overuse.
- Calling a new headache in a 70-year-old "tension" without an **ESR/CRP**.
- Waiting for the temporal artery **biopsy** before steroids → blindness.
- Not checking **fundi**.
- Holding anticoagulation in **CVT** because of a hemorrhagic venous infarct.
- Not asking about **CO** exposure when the whole family has headaches.
- Metoclopramide/prochlorperazine in a **Parkinson** patient.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| 72-year-old, new headache, jaw pain when chewing, transient vision loss | GCA → **high-dose steroids now**, then biopsy |
| Obese young woman, headache, papilledema, CN VI palsy, normal MRI | IIH → MRV, LP (OP high) → acetazolamide |
| Post-partum headache, seizure, MRI hemorrhagic infarct in parasagittal area | CVT → **anticoagulate** |
| Excruciating periorbital pain, tearing, rhinorrhea, nightly, restless | Cluster → **100% O₂** or SC sumatriptan; verapamil prevention |
| Best ED migraine drugs | Dopamine antagonist (prochlorperazine/metoclopramide) ± ketorolac; dexamethasone to prevent recurrence |
| Daily headache, takes acetaminophen-butalbital-caffeine daily | Medication overuse headache |
| Headache worse when upright after spinal anesthesia | Post-dural puncture → blood patch |
| Family with headaches and nausea in winter | CO poisoning → co-oximetry, 100% O₂ |
