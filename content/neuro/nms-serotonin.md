:::bottom
- Fever + confusion + rigidity/twitching + autonomic chaos → **drug-induced hyperthermia** until proven otherwise. Also rule out **sepsis, meningitis**.
- **Stop every culprit drug** (antipsychotics, antiemetics, serotonergic drugs). **Restart missed levodopa/dopamine agonist.**
- **Cool aggressively** (ice packs, cooling blanket, cold IV fluid). **Antipyretics do not work** — this heat is from muscles, not the thermostat.
- **Benzodiazepines** for agitation, rigidity, tremor. **No physical restraints** (isometric contraction = more heat, more rhabdo).
- **Serotonin syndrome** → **cyproheptadine**. **NMS** → **dantrolene** and/or **bromocriptine** if severe.
- Temp **>41 °C** → ICU, sedation, paralysis with a **non-depolarizing** agent, intubation.
- Check **CK, creatinine, K, LFTs, coags** — rhabdo, AKI and DIC kill.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **NMS:** sudden **dopamine block** in the brain (hypothalamus = thermostat; basal ganglia = muscle tone) → **high temperature** + **lead-pipe rigidity** + autonomic instability. Also from **stopping** dopamine drugs (levodopa withdrawal).
- **Serotonin syndrome:** **too much serotonin** stimulation (two serotonergic drugs, a dose increase, or an overdose) → **neuromuscular excitation** (clonus, hyperreflexia, tremor) + autonomic overdrive + agitation.
- Both raise temperature through **muscle activity**, so the fix is to **stop the muscles** — cool, sedate, paralyze — not acetaminophen.

{{fig:hot-four}}

:::analogy
**NMS is a car with the brakes jammed on** — stiff, slow, everything locked, it builds over days. **Serotonin syndrome is a car with the gas pedal stuck** — twitchy, jerky, fast, it starts within hours and stops fast when you take the foot off.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| | Neuroleptic malignant syndrome | Serotonin syndrome |
|---|---|---|
| Drug | **Dopamine antagonist** (haloperidol, any antipsychotic, metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, promethazine) or **stopping levodopa** | **Serotonergic** (SSRI/SNRI, MAOI, linezolid, tramadol, fentanyl, meperidine, methadone, dextromethorphan, triptans (rare), ondansetron, MDMA, lithium, trazodone, St. John's wort, methylene blue) |
| Onset | **Days** (1–3 days after start or dose increase) | **Hours** (<24 h, often <6 h) |
| Muscles | **Lead-pipe rigidity**, bradykinesia | **Clonus** (inducible, ocular), **hyperreflexia**, tremor, legs > arms |
| Reflexes | **Normal or low** | **High** |
| Pupils | Normal | **Dilated** |
| Gut | Normal | **Diarrhea**, hyperactive bowel sounds |
| Mental | Mutism, stupor, catatonia | Agitation, restlessness |
| Labs | **CK very high**, WBC ↑, low iron | CK mildly ↑ (unless severe) |
| Resolves | **~1–2 weeks** (longer with depot) | **~24 h** after stopping drug |
| Antidote | **Bromocriptine**, **dantrolene** | **Cyproheptadine** |

:::mnemonic Serotonin syndrome — Hunter criteria (on a serotonergic drug + ONE of):
- **Spontaneous clonus**
- **Inducible clonus** + agitation or diaphoresis
- **Ocular clonus** + agitation or diaphoresis
- **Tremor + hyperreflexia**
- **Hypertonia + temp >38 °C + ocular or inducible clonus**
:::

### Work-up

- **CK**, BMP (K, creatinine), Mg, phos, Ca, LFTs, **CBC**, coags/fibrinogen, **UA (myoglobin)**, lactate, ECG, blood cultures.
- **LP / CT** if meningitis, encephalitis or bleed is possible (fever + AMS + rigidity overlaps).
- Medication reconciliation **including PRNs and OR/procedural drugs** (methylene blue, fentanyl, ondansetron, metoclopramide).

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Temp >41 °C, severe rigidity, rising CK, unstable?
Y: **ICU.** Intubate; paralyze with **non-depolarizing** agent (rocuronium/vecuronium) — **never succinylcholine** (hyperkalemia with rhabdo). External + internal cooling.
N: Monitored bed. Stop culprit. Benzos. Cool. IV fluids.
Q: Picture fits serotonin syndrome?
Y: **Cyproheptadine** 12 mg PO/NG load, then 2 mg q2h while symptoms persist (max 32 mg/day). Benzos. Avoid antipsychotics.
Q: Picture fits NMS?
Y: Mild: supportive + benzos. Moderate–severe: **bromocriptine** ± **dantrolene**. Severe catatonic NMS not responding → **ECT**.
Q: Parkinson patient whose levodopa was stopped?
Y: **Restart levodopa now** (NG tube if needed) — parkinsonism-hyperpyrexia syndrome.
:::

:::tabs
=== Supportive care for both
- **IV fluids** — target urine output ~200–300 mL/h if CK very high (rhabdo).
- Cooling: ice to groin/axilla, cooling blanket, misting + fans, cold IV fluids.
- **Avoid**: physical restraints, antipyretics as main therapy, succinylcholine, **more antipsychotics**, propranolol (masks tachycardia, hypotension).
- Hypertension: short-acting agents (nicardipine, esmolol). Hypotension in MAOI toxicity: **direct** pressors (norepinephrine, phenylephrine), low dose.
- DVT prophylaxis, aspiration precautions.
=== Restarting the drug
- **Serotonergic drugs:** wait until fully resolved; restart one agent, lower dose; avoid the interacting combination (e.g., no tramadol with SSRI).
- **Antipsychotics after NMS:** wait **≥2 weeks**; choose a **low-potency atypical** (quetiapine, clozapine) at a low dose, titrate slowly, avoid dehydration and IM depot. ~30% recurrence risk.
=== Other hot & confused patients
- **Anticholinergic toxicity:** "hot as a hare, dry as a bone, red as a beet, blind as a bat, mad as a hatter, full as a flask." Benzos; **physostigmine** (with toxicology, if no QRS widening).
- **Malignant hyperthermia:** in OR after **volatile anesthetic or succinylcholine**; rising end-tidal CO₂ first. **Dantrolene 2.5 mg/kg** IV; MHAUS hotline.
- **Sympathomimetic** (cocaine, meth): sweaty, big pupils, **no clonus** → benzos.
- **Thyroid storm**, **heat stroke**, **sepsis/meningitis**, **alcohol/benzo withdrawal**, **lethal catatonia**.
:::

:::dose
- **Cyproheptadine:** 12 mg PO/NG, then 2 mg q2h while symptomatic (or 8 mg q6h maintenance). Oral only (crush). Sedating.
- **Lorazepam:** 1–2 mg IV q15–30 min to effect; diazepam alternative.
- **Bromocriptine:** 2.5 mg PO/NG q8h, up to 5 mg q4h (max ~40 mg/day). Continue ~10 days after resolution.
- **Dantrolene:** 1–2.5 mg/kg IV, repeat up to 10 mg/kg/day; watch LFTs. Do not combine with calcium-channel blockers (cardiovascular collapse).
- **Amantadine** 100 mg PO q8h is an alternative to bromocriptine.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- **Haloperidol for the agitated, febrile patient** who actually has NMS or serotonin syndrome → worse.
- Missing **linezolid + SSRI**, **methylene blue + SSRI** (in the OR), **tramadol/fentanyl + SSRI**, **ondansetron** in heavy serotonergic regimens.
- Waiting for "fever" in NMS — temperature can start normal; rising CK and rigidity come first.
- Using acetaminophen and calling it treatment.
- Physical restraints → more heat, more CK.
- Forgetting **levodopa withdrawal** in an NPO Parkinson patient.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Haloperidol 2 days ago; T 40 °C, rigid, CK 25,000, mute | NMS → stop drug, cool, benzos, bromocriptine/dantrolene |
| Sertraline + new tramadol; hours later agitation, diarrhea, ankle clonus | Serotonin syndrome → stop drugs, benzos, **cyproheptadine** |
| On SSRI, MRSA pneumonia treated with linezolid, now tremor and hyperreflexia | Serotonin syndrome (linezolid is an MAOI) |
| Hot, dry, flushed skin, big pupils, urinary retention, no bowel sounds | **Anticholinergic** toxidrome |
| Rising EtCO₂ and rigidity minutes after sevoflurane | **Malignant hyperthermia** → dantrolene |
| Best distinguishing exam: NMS vs SS | **Clonus/hyperreflexia** (SS) vs **lead-pipe rigidity, slow reflexes** (NMS) |
| Antipyretic for NMS hyperthermia? | Not effective — use **physical cooling** |
| Parkinson patient, NPO 2 days, fever, rigidity | Parkinsonism-hyperpyrexia → **restart levodopa** |
