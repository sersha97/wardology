:::bottom
- Ask **timing and triggers** (TiTrATE), not "spinning or light-headed?"
- **Continuous** vertigo for hours–days + nausea + nystagmus + unsteady = **acute vestibular syndrome (AVS)**: **vestibular neuritis or posterior circulation stroke**.
- In AVS, do **HINTS + gait** (only if trained and the patient has **nystagmus**). Any **dangerous** sign → **MRI with DWI** (CT misses most posterior strokes).
- **Can't stand or walk without help** = central until proven otherwise.
- **Brief, positional** (<1 min, lying down/rolling over) = **BPPV** → **Dix-Hallpike** → **Epley maneuver**. No meclizine needed long term.
- Sudden vertigo + **new hearing loss** → stroke (AICA) or labyrinthitis → **urgent MRI** and ENT.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Balance = **inner ear** (vestibular) + **eyes** + **joint sense**, blended in the **brainstem and cerebellum**.
- One inner ear suddenly goes quiet (**vestibular neuritis**) → the brain thinks the head is turning → vertigo + nystagmus **away** from the bad ear.
- A **cerebellar or brainstem stroke** can look exactly the same. ~**10%** of acute dizziness in the ED is central; small posterior strokes often have **no** limb weakness or slurred speech.
- **BPPV:** loose otoconia ("ear crystals") fall into a **semicircular canal** (usually posterior). Head moves → crystals roll → canal fires → brief spin.

:::analogy
The inner ears are **two microphones** that should send the same volume to the brain. In vestibular neuritis one microphone **cuts out** — the brain hears silence on one side and thinks the room is turning. In BPPV a few **pebbles roll around in the microphone** when you tilt it.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

### HINTS — only in a patient with ongoing vertigo + spontaneous nystagmus

| Test | **Peripheral** (reassuring) | **Central** (dangerous) |
|---|---|---|
| **H**ead **I**mpulse | **Abnormal**: corrective catch-up saccade toward one side | **Normal** (no saccade) — in a dizzy patient this is **bad** |
| **N**ystagmus | **One direction**, horizontal, better with fixation | **Direction-changing** with gaze, **vertical** or torsional |
| **T**est of **S**kew | No vertical eye shift | **Skew deviation** (one eye moves up/down on cover) |
| **Gait** (+ "HINTS plus": new hearing loss) | Walks, leans | **Cannot stand/walk** unaided |

:::mnemonic INFARCT — any one = central
**I**mpulse **N**ormal · **F**ast-phase **A**lternating · **R**efixation on **C**over **T**est (skew). Add **new hearing loss** (HINTS plus — AICA stroke).
:::

:::warn HINTS works only if…
…the patient has **continuous** vertigo **with nystagmus right now**, and the examiner is trained. A normal head impulse in a patient **without** vertigo or nystagmus means nothing. Not trained or unsure → **MRI with DWI** (or neurology).
:::

### Imaging

- **CT head** is poor for posterior stroke (sensitivity ~20–40%) — use only to rule out bleed.
- **MRI with DWI**: best test, but can be **falsely negative in the first 24–48 h** (~15–20% for small posterior strokes). Strong clinical suspicion → repeat MRI or treat as stroke.
- **CTA head and neck:** vertebral dissection/stenosis (neck pain, trauma).

:::tabs
=== Dix-Hallpike & Epley
**Dix-Hallpike** (posterior canal BPPV — ~85–90%):
1. Patient sits; turn head **45°** toward the tested ear.
2. Lie back quickly, head hanging **20°** below the bed.
3. Positive: after a few seconds' delay, **upbeating + torsional** nystagmus toward the lower ear, lasting **<60 s**, fatigues with repeats.

**Epley** (treat the affected side): from the positive Hallpike position → turn head 90° to the other side (30–60 s) → roll onto that side with nose down (30–60 s) → sit up. Repeat until no nystagmus. Works in ~80% after 1–3 cycles.

**Supine roll test** if Hallpike negative — horizontal canal BPPV (geotropic/apogeotropic nystagmus) → **barbecue roll** / Gufoni.
=== Peripheral causes
| | Clue |
|---|---|
| **Vestibular neuritis** | Days of vertigo after a viral illness, no hearing loss, positive head impulse |
| **Labyrinthitis** | Same + **hearing loss** (still image if any doubt) |
| **BPPV** | Seconds, positional, older adults |
| **Ménière** | Episodes 20 min–12 h + **fluctuating low-frequency hearing loss + tinnitus + ear fullness** |
| **Vestibular migraine** | Episodes minutes–days, migraine history, light sensitivity |
| **Drug toxicity** | Aminoglycosides (bilateral, oscillopsia), anticonvulsants, alcohol |
=== Central red flags
- **Cannot walk** · new **headache/neck pain** (dissection) · **diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, numbness, Horner** · **vertical or direction-changing** nystagmus · **sudden deafness** · vascular risk factors · **recurrent** spells before (TIA).
:::

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Brief, triggered by head position, normal exam between spells?
Y: **Dix-Hallpike** → if positive, **Epley**. Vestibular rehab handout. No routine imaging.
Q: Continuous vertigo with nystagmus (AVS)?
Y: **HINTS + gait** (trained examiner). Any central sign, cannot walk, or unsure → **MRI with DWI**, stroke pathway (lysis/EVT if within window, see Stroke).
Q: Peripheral HINTS and walks?
Y: **Vestibular neuritis** → short course of symptom relief (≤2–3 days), **early walking**, vestibular rehab; **prednisone** optional within 72 h (modest effect).
Q: New hearing loss?
Y: Urgent MRI (AICA stroke) + audiology/ENT. Sudden sensorineural hearing loss → **steroids** within 2 weeks.
:::

:::dose
- **Meclizine:** 25 mg PO q6–8h PRN, **≤2–3 days** (delays central compensation; anticholinergic — avoid in elderly).
- **Ondansetron:** 4 mg IV/PO q8h PRN nausea.
- **Diazepam:** 2–5 mg PO short term for severe vertigo (falls risk).
- **Prednisone** (vestibular neuritis, optional): ~1 mg/kg (max 60 mg) taper over ~10 days if started early.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- "**Light-headed vs spinning**" — unreliable. Ask **timing and triggers**.
- Doing HINTS in a patient **without nystagmus** and calling a normal head impulse "reassuring" — it is the opposite.
- **Negative CT** = "no stroke."
- Discharging a patient who **cannot walk**.
- Days of **meclizine** for BPPV instead of an Epley.
- Missing **vertebral dissection** after neck manipulation or trauma.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| 30 s of spinning when rolling over in bed, torsional upbeat nystagmus on Hallpike | BPPV → **Epley** |
| 2 days of constant vertigo after URI, abnormal head impulse, unidirectional nystagmus, walks | Vestibular neuritis |
| Constant vertigo, **normal** head impulse, direction-changing nystagmus | **Cerebellar stroke** → MRI DWI |
| Vertigo + sudden unilateral deafness, vascular risk | **AICA stroke** (or labyrinthitis) → MRI |
| Vertigo, hoarseness, ipsilateral Horner, face numbness, contralateral body pain/temp loss | **Wallenberg** (lateral medulla, PICA/vertebral) |
| Episodes of vertigo, low-frequency hearing loss, tinnitus, ear fullness | **Ménière** → salt restriction, diuretics |
| Best imaging for suspected posterior stroke | **MRI with DWI** |
