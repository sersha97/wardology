:::bottom
- Fever + headache + stiff neck + confusion (most have **≥2 of 4**) → suspect bacterial meningitis.
- **Blood cultures ×2 → dexamethasone → antibiotics** within **30–60 minutes**. **Do not wait for CT or LP.**
- Empiric adult regimen: **ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12h + vancomycin**. Add **ampicillin 2 g IV q4h** if **age >50**, pregnant, alcohol use, or immunosuppressed (**Listeria**).
- **Dexamethasone 10 mg IV q6h × 4 days**, first dose with or just before antibiotics. Stop if not pneumococcal.
- **CT before LP** only if: immunocompromised, known CNS lesion, new seizure, papilledema, focal deficit, or GCS ≤10.
- Suspected meningococcus → **droplet precautions** for 24 h and **chemoprophylaxis** for close contacts. Notify public health.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Bacteria colonize the nose/throat → enter the blood → cross into the **subarachnoid space**, where there are few defenses (low complement, few antibodies).
- Killed bacteria release cell-wall pieces → huge **inflammation** → brain swelling, vasculitis, stroke, hydrocephalus, deafness. **Inflammation, not the bug alone, causes much of the damage** — that is why dexamethasone helps (mainly pneumococcus).
- **Adults:** *S. pneumoniae* (most common, highest mortality) · *N. meningitidis* (young, dorms, petechial rash) · ***Listeria*** (>50, pregnancy, alcohol, immunosuppressed) · *H. influenzae* (now rare).
- **After neurosurgery / shunt / head trauma:** *Staph aureus*, coagulase-negative staph, **gram-negatives incl. Pseudomonas**.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

### Exam

- **Neck stiffness**, **Kernig/Brudzinski** — low sensitivity; a negative test does **not** rule out meningitis.
- **Jolt accentuation** (headache worse with horizontal head turning) — sensitive in some studies, not reliable alone.
- **Petechiae / purpura** = meningococcemia → antibiotics **immediately**, ICU.
- Look for a source: otitis, sinusitis, pneumonia, endocarditis (murmur), CSF leak.

### CSF — read it like this

| | Bacterial | Viral | TB / fungal | Normal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening pressure | **High** (>25 cmH₂O) | Normal / mildly ↑ | **High** (crypto: very high) | 10–20 |
| WBC (/µL) | **1,000–5,000**, neutrophils | 10–500, **lymphocytes** | 50–500, lymphocytes | <5 |
| Protein (mg/dL) | **High** (>100–200) | Normal / mildly ↑ | **High** | 15–45 |
| Glucose (CSF:serum) | **Low** (<0.4) | Normal | **Low** | ~0.6 |

- Send: **cell count + diff (tubes 1 and 4)**, protein, glucose (with **serum glucose**), **Gram stain**, culture, **multiplex PCR panel** (BioFire ME), HSV PCR, cryptococcal antigen if immunosuppressed, lactate (>3.5 mmol/L favors bacterial).
- **Early viral meningitis** can be neutrophil-predominant. **Partially treated** bacterial meningitis can look viral. If unsure → **keep treating** and repeat LP.

:::pearl Do not let the CT delay the drugs
Most adults do **not** need CT before LP (Hasbun 2001). If a CT is needed, the order is **cultures → dex → antibiotics → CT → LP**. Antibiotics before LP lower culture yield over hours, but Gram stain, PCR, antigen and CSF cells still help.
:::

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Suspected bacterial meningitis?
Y: Blood cultures → **dexamethasone 10 mg IV** → **ceftriaxone 2 g IV + vancomycin** (± **ampicillin**). Then CT if needed, then LP.
Q: Age >50, pregnant, alcohol, cancer, transplant, steroids, diabetes?
Y: Add **ampicillin 2 g IV q4h** (*Listeria* is resistant to cephalosporins).
Q: Recent neurosurgery, shunt, penetrating trauma?
Y: **Vancomycin + cefepime (or ceftazidime or meropenem)**. No routine dexamethasone.
Q: Severe beta-lactam allergy?
Y: **Vancomycin + moxifloxacin** (or aztreonam/meropenem per allergy) + **TMP-SMX** for *Listeria*. Call ID.
Q: Gram stain / culture back?
Y: Narrow: pneumococcus (continue dex, adjust by MIC) · meningococcus (penicillin or ceftriaxone, **stop dex**) · *Listeria* (ampicillin ± gentamicin, 21 days).
:::

:::tabs
=== Duration
| Organism | Duration |
|---|---|
| *N. meningitidis*, *H. influenzae* | 7 days |
| *S. pneumoniae* | 10–14 days |
| *Listeria* | ≥21 days |
| Gram-negative bacilli | 21 days |
=== Complications to watch
- **Raised ICP / herniation**, **seizures**, **hydrocephalus** (CT/neurosurgery), stroke (vasculitis), **SIADH** (check Na), venous sinus thrombosis.
- **Hearing loss** — audiology before discharge (especially pneumococcus).
- Meningococcemia: **DIC**, purpura fulminans, adrenal hemorrhage (Waterhouse–Friderichsen).
- Not improving at 48 h → repeat LP / imaging; look for abscess, empyema, resistant organism.
=== Chemoprophylaxis (meningococcus)
- **Who:** household, daycare, kissing/sharing drinks, **anyone exposed to oral secretions** (e.g., **intubation without a mask**). Not routine for all staff.
- **What:** **ciprofloxacin 500 mg PO once** · or **rifampin 600 mg PO q12h × 2 days** · or **ceftriaxone 250 mg IM once** (pregnancy).
- **When:** ideally within 24 h of diagnosis.
- Patient: droplet precautions for **24 h** after effective antibiotics.
:::

:::dose
- **Dexamethasone:** 0.15 mg/kg (≈10 mg) IV q6h × 4 days, start with or 10–20 min before (up to ~4 h after) the first antibiotic dose.
- **Ceftriaxone:** 2 g IV q12h (CNS dose).
- **Vancomycin:** 15–20 mg/kg IV q8–12h, AUC-guided (aim higher troughs/AUC for CNS); load 25–30 mg/kg if severe.
- **Ampicillin:** 2 g IV q4h.
- **Cefepime:** 2 g IV q8h · **Meropenem:** 2 g IV q8h.
- Renal adjustments: pharmacy.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- **CT before antibiotics.** The #1 delay.
- Forgetting **ampicillin** in the 70-year-old or the transplant patient.
- Using ceftriaxone **1 g daily** (non-CNS dose).
- Giving dexamethasone **hours after** antibiotics or continuing it for meningococcus/Listeria.
- Ruling out meningitis because there is **no neck stiffness**.
- Ignoring a **rash** — petechiae = meningococcemia, minutes matter.
- Not checking **serum glucose** with CSF glucose.
- Forgetting **contact prophylaxis** and public-health notification.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| 20-year-old college student, fever, petechial rash, stiff neck | Meningococcus → ceftriaxone now; droplet precautions; contacts prophylaxis |
| 72-year-old, fever, confusion, CSF neutrophils, gram-positive rods | ***Listeria*** → ampicillin (± gentamicin) |
| Empiric therapy, 35-year-old healthy adult | Ceftriaxone + vancomycin + dexamethasone |
| Empiric therapy, 60-year-old | Ceftriaxone + vancomycin + **ampicillin** + dexamethasone |
| When to do CT before LP | Immunocompromised, CNS disease, seizure, papilledema, focal deficit, GCS ≤10 |
| Dexamethasone benefit mostly in which bug? | *S. pneumoniae* (↓ death and hearing loss) |
| Post-craniotomy meningitis | Vancomycin + cefepime/ceftazidime/meropenem |
| CSF: very high opening pressure, lymphocytes, low glucose, HIV CD4 40 | Cryptococcus → CrAg; amphotericin + flucytosine; **serial LPs** |
| ICU nurse intubated a meningococcal patient without a mask | Ciprofloxacin 500 mg ×1 |
