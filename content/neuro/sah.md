:::bottom
- **Thunderclap headache** (maximal within 1 minute) = SAH until proven otherwise. Also: exertion/sex onset, syncope, neck pain, vomiting, seizure.
- **Non-contrast CT head now.** Within **6 h** of onset (modern CT, expert read) → negative CT rules out SAH.
- CT **>6 h** and negative → **LP** (xanthochromia) **or CTA**.
- **Confirmed aneurysmal SAH:** neurosurgery/neuro-ICU, **SBP <160** until the aneurysm is secured, **reverse anticoagulation**, **nimodipine 60 mg q4h × 21 days**, **secure the aneurysm within 24 h**.
- Days **3–14**: watch for **vasospasm / delayed cerebral ischemia**, **hydrocephalus**, **hyponatremia** (cerebral salt wasting) and rebleed. **Keep euvolemic.**
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **~85%** of spontaneous SAH = ruptured **saccular (berry) aneurysm** at the circle of Willis (ACom, PCom, MCA bifurcation). Others: perimesencephalic (benign), AVM, dissection, RCVS, trauma (most common SAH overall).
- Blood under pressure fills the subarachnoid space → **ICP spikes** → brief global ischemia (syncope), **chemical meningitis** (neck stiffness), blocked CSF flow (**hydrocephalus**).
- Blood breakdown products irritate arteries → **vasospasm** days 3–14 → **delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI)**.
- Risk factors: **smoking**, **hypertension**, cocaine/stimulants, family history (2 first-degree relatives), **ADPKD**, Ehlers-Danlos, coarctation.

:::analogy
An aneurysm is a weak bubble on a garden hose. When it pops, high-pressure water floods the yard (subarachnoid space). For the next two weeks the hose itself **cramps** (vasospasm) and the drain gets **clogged** (hydrocephalus). The first job is to **patch the bubble** before it pops again.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

{{fig:sah-path}}

:::mnemonic Ottawa SAH rule — image if ANY (alert, age ≥15, new severe atraumatic headache peaking ≤1 h)
- Age **≥40**
- **Neck pain or stiffness**
- Witnessed **loss of consciousness**
- Onset during **exertion**
- **Thunderclap** (instant peak)
- **Limited neck flexion** on exam

All negative → SAH very unlikely (sensitivity ~100%, specificity low).
:::

### LP for SAH

- Ideally **≥6–12 h** after onset (xanthochromia needs time to form).
- **Xanthochromia** (yellow supernatant, bilirubin) = blood was there before the needle.
- **RBCs that stay high from tube 1 to tube 4** favor SAH; **falling** counts favor traumatic tap (not reliable alone).
- Measure **opening pressure** (high in SAH, CVT, IIH).

### Grading (tell neurosurgery)

- **Hunt-Hess / WFNS** (clinical): 1 = mild headache … 5 = coma.
- **Modified Fisher** (CT): thick blood + intraventricular blood = highest vasospasm risk.

### Thunderclap with negative SAH work-up

| Cause | Clue | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **RCVS** | Recurrent thunderclaps over days; post-partum, SSRIs, decongestants, cannabis | CTA/MRA: "string of beads"; normal at first in some |
| **Cervical artery dissection** | Neck pain, Horner, trauma/chiropractor | CTA neck |
| **Cerebral venous thrombosis** | Progressive, papilledema, seizure, pregnancy/OCP | CT/MR venogram |
| **Pituitary apoplexy** | Visual loss, ophthalmoplegia, **hypotension** | MRI; give **stress-dose steroids** |
| **Spontaneous intracranial hypotension** | Worse upright, better lying | MRI with contrast |
| Hypertensive emergency / PRES | Very high BP, visual symptoms | MRI |

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: SAH on CT or LP?
Y: **CTA** (find aneurysm) · neurosurgery + neuro-ICU · transfer if no capability.
Q: Aneurysm not yet secured?
Y: **SBP <160** (nicardipine or clevidipine) · **reverse anticoagulation** · hold antiplatelets · bedrest, pain control, stool softener, antiemetic · avoid hypotension.
Q: Secure the aneurysm within 24 h?
Y: **Endovascular coiling** (preferred when both possible) or **surgical clipping**.
Q: Drowsy, enlarging ventricles?
Y: **External ventricular drain (EVD)** — acute hydrocephalus.
Q: Day 3–14: new focal deficit or drop in consciousness?
Y: Think **DCI/vasospasm** → keep euvolemic, induce **hypertension** (secured aneurysm), CT perfusion/angio, endovascular therapy.
:::

:::tabs
=== Ongoing ICU care
- **Nimodipine 60 mg PO/NG q4h × 21 days** (improves outcome; does not prevent spasm on angiography). If BP drops → **30 mg q2h**.
- **Euvolemia** (no prophylactic "triple H").
- **Sodium:** check twice daily. Hyponatremia is often **cerebral salt wasting** → isotonic/hypertonic saline, **fludrocortisone**; **do not fluid restrict** (raises DCI risk).
- **Seizures:** treat if they occur; short prophylaxis only (avoid phenytoin).
- **Glucose** <180, **normothermia**, **DVT prophylaxis**: SCDs now, heparin 24 h after aneurysm secured.
- **TCD** monitoring (rising velocities = spasm).
- Cardiac: **stunned myocardium** (takotsubo), troponin rise, deep T inversions, long QT — neurogenic, usually recovers.
=== Unruptured aneurysm found incidentally
- Neurosurgery/neurointerventional follow-up; risk scores (**PHASES**).
- Stop **smoking**, control BP, avoid stimulants.
- Screen first-degree relatives if ≥2 family members affected or ADPKD.
:::

:::dose
- **Nimodipine:** 60 mg PO/NG q4h × 21 days (30 mg q2h if hypotension). **Oral only** — never IV (cardiac arrest).
- **Nicardipine:** 5 mg/h IV, titrate by 2.5 mg/h q5–15 min (max 15 mg/h).
- **Reversal:** warfarin → 4F-PCC + vitamin K 10 mg IV · Xa inhibitor → **4F-PCC** (andexanet withdrawn in US, Dec 2025) · dabigatran → idarucizumab.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- "Migraine" in a patient whose headache **peaked instantly** — always ask *how fast did it start?*
- Opioids/antiemetics making the headache better → false reassurance. Response to treatment does not exclude SAH.
- CT done **>6 h** after onset and called negative without LP or CTA.
- **Fluid restriction** for hyponatremia after SAH → vasospasm.
- IV nimodipine — dangerous.
- Missing **sentinel headache** days before a major bleed.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Sudden worst headache during sex, now 2 h ago, alert | Non-contrast CT head |
| CT negative at 3 h, normal neuro exam | SAH ruled out (no LP needed with modern CT) |
| CT negative at 12 h | LP (xanthochromia) or CTA |
| Day 7 after SAH, new aphasia | Vasospasm / DCI → induced hypertension, angiography |
| Day 5 after SAH, Na 127, low volume, high urine Na | **Cerebral salt wasting** → saline (not fluid restriction) |
| Drug that improves outcome after SAH | **Nimodipine** |
| Post-partum woman, recurrent thunderclaps, string-of-beads angiogram | **RCVS** → stop vasoactive drugs, calcium-channel blocker, no steroids |
| ADPKD patient, sudden headache | Ruptured berry aneurysm |
| Sudden headache + visual loss + hypotension, pituitary mass | Pituitary apoplexy → **steroids** |
