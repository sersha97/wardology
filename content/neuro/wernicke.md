:::bottom
- **Thiamine deficiency** → acute brain injury. Classic triad: **confusion + eye signs + ataxia** — but only **~10–20%** have all three.
- **Treat on suspicion:** any **one** sign + a risk factor (alcohol, vomiting, poor intake, bariatric surgery, cancer, dialysis, refeeding).
- **Thiamine 500 mg IV q8h × 3 days**, then **250 mg IV daily × 3–5 days**, then **100 mg PO daily** long term.
- Give thiamine **before or with glucose** — but **never delay glucose** in hypoglycemia.
- Replace **magnesium** (thiamine does not work well when Mg is low).
- Missed or under-dosed → **Korsakoff** (permanent amnesia, confabulation) or death.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **Thiamine (B1)** is a cofactor for enzymes that make **ATP from glucose** (pyruvate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, transketolase).
- Body stores are small (**~18 days**). Poor intake, vomiting, malabsorption or high demand empties them quickly.
- Without thiamine, the busiest brain areas fail first: **mammillary bodies, medial thalamus, periaqueductal gray, cerebellar vermis** → confusion, eye-movement problems, ataxia.
- **A glucose load** without thiamine burns the last thiamine → can **precipitate** Wernicke.

:::analogy
Glucose is the **fuel**, thiamine is the **spark plug**. Pour fuel into an engine without spark plugs and nothing runs — and the last spark plug burns out. So you put in the spark plug first (or at the same time).
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

### Who is at risk (not only alcohol)

- **Alcohol use disorder** (most common).
- **Hyperemesis gravidarum**, persistent vomiting, **bariatric surgery**, GI obstruction, malabsorption.
- **Malnutrition**, anorexia, cancer, AIDS, prolonged NPO / **TPN without vitamins**.
- **Refeeding** after starvation; **dialysis**; high-dose diuretics; thyrotoxicosis.

### Caine criteria (alcohol use disorder) — 2 of 4

1. **Dietary deficiency** (malnutrition)
2. **Eye signs** — nystagmus (most common), lateral rectus (CN VI) palsy, conjugate gaze palsy
3. **Cerebellar dysfunction** — gait/truncal ataxia
4. **Altered mental status or mild memory impairment**

### Tests

- **Clinical diagnosis.** Do **not** wait for tests.
- Thiamine level (whole blood, **draw before treatment**) — slow, not needed to treat.
- **MRI:** symmetric T2/FLAIR signal in **medial thalami**, mammillary bodies, periaqueductal gray (~50% sensitive). Normal MRI does **not** rule out.
- Also check: **Mg**, phos, K, glucose, LFTs, lactate (lactic acidosis can be thiamine-related), B12/folate.

:::tabs
=== Korsakoff syndrome
- Chronic result of untreated or under-treated Wernicke.
- **Anterograde (and retrograde) amnesia** + **confabulation**; attention and other cognition relatively spared.
- Mostly **irreversible**; ~20% improve with long-term thiamine and abstinence.
=== Other thiamine-deficiency pictures
- **Wet beriberi:** high-output **heart failure**, lactic acidosis, vasodilation (Shoshin beriberi = fulminant, shock).
- **Dry beriberi:** symmetric peripheral neuropathy.
- Unexplained **lactic acidosis** in a malnourished or septic patient → give thiamine.
:::

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Confused, ataxic or eye signs + risk factor?
Y: **Thiamine 500 mg IV q8h** (infuse over 30 min) — start now, before imaging.
Q: Hypoglycemic?
Y: Give **dextrose now** and thiamine at the same time — never delay glucose.
Q: Mg low?
Y: Replace **magnesium** (thiamine-dependent enzymes need Mg).
Q: Improving after 3 days?
Y: Step down to **250 mg IV daily × 3–5 days** (or until no further improvement), then **100 mg PO daily**. Address alcohol use (see Alcohol withdrawal).
N: Continue high dose; reconsider diagnosis (MRI, EEG, other causes of AMS).
:::

:::pearl Prophylaxis for at-risk patients without signs
**Thiamine 100–250 mg IV/IM daily × 3–5 days** (IV preferred if absorption doubtful), then oral. Every **alcohol withdrawal** admission, every **refeeding-risk** patient, every **hyperemesis** patient.
:::

:::dose
- **Treatment:** thiamine 500 mg IV q8h × 3 days → 250 mg IV daily × 3–5 days → 100 mg PO daily.
- **Prophylaxis:** 100–250 mg IV/IM daily.
- **Magnesium sulfate:** 2–4 g IV, repeat per level.
- Anaphylaxis with IV thiamine is **very rare** — the benefit is far greater.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Waiting for the **full triad**.
- **100 mg PO** for suspected Wernicke — oral absorption is poor in alcohol use; use **high-dose IV**.
- Thinking only of alcohol — missing it in **hyperemesis**, **post-bariatric**, cancer, TPN.
- Attributing confusion to "intoxication" or "withdrawal" alone.
- Forgetting **magnesium**.
- Stopping thiamine at discharge.
