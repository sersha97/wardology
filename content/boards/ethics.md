:::bottom
- **Capacity** (clinical, decision-specific) = communicate a choice, **understand, appreciate, reason**. Dementia or psychiatric illness alone ≠ incapacity.
- No capacity → **advance directive** → **health care proxy** → surrogate hierarchy. Surrogates use **substituted judgment**, then best interest.
- **Disclose errors** promptly: what happened, apology, consequences, prevention. Report through the safety system.
- Competence is a **legal** determination by a court.
:::

## 🔎 Classic board scenarios

| Scenario | Answer |
|---|---|
| Capable patient refuses life-saving treatment | **Respect** it; explore reasons; document |
| Family wants "everything" against the patient's directive | **Follow the directive / proxy** |
| No surrogate, emergency | Treat under **implied consent** |
| Minor (<18) | Parents decide, except emancipated minors and confidential care (STIs, contraception, pregnancy, mental health/substance use per state law) |
| Jehovah's Witness adult refuses blood | Respect; for a child in an emergency, transfuse (court order if time) |
| Patient asks to keep a diagnosis from family | Respect confidentiality |
| Colleague impaired | Report (institution, physician health program) |
| Gift from patient | Small gifts OK; decline large or influencing ones |

## 🧠 Futility & withdrawal in 60 seconds

- Withholding and withdrawing treatment are **ethically equivalent**.
- **Double effect**: opioids to relieve dyspnea/pain at end of life are acceptable even if they might hasten death.
- Physicians are not obliged to provide **physiologically futile** treatment, but follow institutional processes.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **Interpreters**: use professional interpreters, not family members.
- Duty to warn (Tarasoff) — specific credible threat to an identifiable person.
- Patients have a right to their records.
:::
