:::bottom
- **Back pain + cancer** → think **cord compression**, even before deficits.
- Deficits → **dexamethasone now** + **emergency MRI of the whole spine** → radiation ± surgery within hours.
- **Hypercalcemia of malignancy** → IV saline, calcitonin, then zoledronic acid (or denosumab).
- **SVC syndrome** → get a tissue diagnosis first unless the airway or brain is threatened; stent for severe symptoms.
- **Tumor lysis** → aggressive IV fluids, allopurinol or rasburicase, watch K⁺, phosphate and calcium.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Cord compression usually comes from a **vertebral body metastasis** growing back into the epidural space; venous congestion and edema → ischemia of the cord.
- **Thoracic spine** ~60%. Lung, breast and prostate cancer cause most cases.

## 🔎 Recognize it

| Stage | Clue |
|---|---|
| Early | Back pain worse **lying down**, at night, with cough or Valsalva |
| Radicular | Band-like chest or abdominal pain |
| Late | Leg weakness, **sensory level**, hyperreflexia, urinary retention, constipation |

## 💊 Management

- Dexamethasone (for example 10 mg IV, then 4 mg every 6 hours), analgesia, VTE prophylaxis.
- **Surgery + radiation** beats radiation alone for single-level compression with good performance status (Patchell trial).
- Radiation alone for radiosensitive tumors (lymphoma, myeloma, small cell, seminoma) or poor surgical candidates.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **Ambulatory status at treatment predicts outcome**: most who walk in keep walking.
- Hypercalcemia: **PTHrP** high with squamous cancers; **1,25-vitamin D** high with lymphoma.
- Tumor lysis: **hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, hypocalcemia** — rasburicase is contraindicated in **G6PD deficiency**.
:::
