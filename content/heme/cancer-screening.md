:::bottom
- **Breast**: mammography every **2 years, ages 40–74** (average risk).
- **Cervical**: cytology every 3 years at 21–29; at 30–65 **HPV every 5 years** (or cotest q5y or cytology q3y).
- **Colorectal**: start at **45**; first-degree relative <60 → colonoscopy at 40 (or 10 years earlier) every 5 years.
- **Lung**: annual **low-dose CT**, ages **50–80**, **≥20 pack-years**, smoking now or quit **≤15 years**.
- **Prostate**: shared decision about PSA at **55–69**; not after 70.
:::

## 🧠 Why screening rules matter

- Screening helps only if the disease has a detectable preclinical phase, treatment works better early, and harms (false positives, overdiagnosis) are acceptable.
- **Lead-time bias** and **length-time bias** make screening look better than it is — trials must show lower mortality.

## 🔎 Colorectal options (average risk, 45–75)

| Test | Interval |
|---|---|
| Colonoscopy | 10 years |
| FIT | 1 year |
| Stool DNA–FIT | 1–3 years |
| CT colonography / flexible sigmoidoscopy | 5 years |

Any positive stool or CT test → **colonoscopy**. Ages 76–85: individualize.

## 🔎 Higher-risk groups

- Breast: lifetime risk ≥20%, BRCA, chest radiation → **annual MRI + mammography**.
- Colorectal: Lynch syndrome → colonoscopy every 1–2 years from 20–25; IBD colitis → surveillance after 8 years.
- Cervical: HIV, transplant, prior CIN2+ → more frequent screening, continue past 65.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Stop cervical screening after **hysterectomy for benign disease** (cervix removed, no CIN2+ history).
- Stop colorectal screening around **75** if prior screening was negative; do not start after 85.
- Do not screen for ovarian, pancreatic, thyroid or testicular cancer in average-risk adults.
:::
