:::bottom
- **Microhematuria** = ≥3 RBC/hpf on microscopy. High risk (age ≥60, smoker >30 pack-years, >25 RBC/hpf, dye/rubber exposure) → **cystoscopy + CT urography**.
- **Painless intratesticular mass** → **scrotal ultrasound** → markers (AFP, β-hCG, LDH) → **inguinal orchiectomy**. Never scrotal biopsy.
- **Torsion** → surgery within 6 hours; do not wait for imaging if classic.
- **PDE-5 inhibitors + nitrates = contraindicated.**
:::

## 🔎 Scrotal findings

| Finding | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Firm, painless, in testis | **Cancer** |
| Transilluminates, around testis | Hydrocele |
| "Bag of worms", left, worse standing | Varicocele (new right-sided → look for mass) |
| Cyst above testis | Spermatocele |
| Pain, fever, Prehn sign + | Epididymitis (STI <35; enteric >35) |
| Sudden pain, high-riding testis, no cremasteric reflex | **Torsion** |

## 🔎 Erectile dysfunction

- Often **vascular** — a CVD warning sign; check BP, lipids, glucose.
- Check **morning testosterone** if low libido.
- Drugs: thiazides, beta-blockers, SSRIs, spironolactone, opioids.
- First-line PDE-5 inhibitor — unless **nitrates** (or riociguat). Alternatives: vacuum device, intracavernosal alprostadil, prosthesis.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Anticoagulation does **not** explain hematuria — evaluate anyway.
- Dipstick-positive, microscopy-negative → myoglobin or hemoglobin (rhabdomyolysis, hemolysis).
:::
