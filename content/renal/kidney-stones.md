:::bottom
- Acute colic: **NSAID** for pain; **tamsulosin** helps pass distal stones 5–10 mm.
- **Infection + obstruction** = emergency decompression (stent or nephrostomy).
- Everyone: urine volume **>2.5 L/day**, **normal calcium**, **low sodium**, moderate animal protein.
- Recurrent stones → **24-hour urine** and stone analysis → targeted drug.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Stones form when urine is supersaturated: too much solute (calcium, oxalate, uric acid) or too little water or inhibitor (**citrate**).
- **Calcium oxalate** ≈ 80%. Low dietary calcium → more free oxalate absorbed → more stones.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

- **Noncontrast low-dose CT** is the most sensitive test; ultrasound first in pregnancy and young patients.
- Serum calcium (exclude hyperparathyroidism), uric acid, creatinine; strain urine for stone analysis.

## 💊 Prevention by finding

| 24-h urine finding | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Hypercalciuria | Thiazide + sodium restriction |
| Hypocitraturia | Potassium citrate |
| Hyperuricosuria (calcium stones) | Allopurinol |
| Uric acid stones | **Alkalinize urine** (potassium citrate); allopurinol if still forming |
| Enteric hyperoxaluria (Crohn, bariatric surgery) | Low-fat, low-oxalate diet, calcium with meals |
| Cystine stones | Fluids, alkalinization, tiopronin |

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **Struvite** (staghorn) stones = urease-producing bacteria (*Proteus*) → surgical removal.
- **Uric acid stones are radiolucent** on plain films but visible on CT.
- Topiramate and acetazolamide cause calcium phosphate stones.
:::
