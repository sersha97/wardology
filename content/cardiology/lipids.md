:::bottom
- **Clinical ASCVD** → **high-intensity statin**. Very high risk with LDL ≥70 mg/dL on max statin → add **ezetimibe**, then a **PCSK9 inhibitor**.
- **LDL ≥190 mg/dL** → high-intensity statin, no risk calculator needed; think familial hypercholesterolemia.
- **Diabetes, age 40–75** → at least moderate-intensity statin.
- **Primary prevention, 40–75** → estimate 10-year risk and discuss; use risk enhancers and **coronary calcium** when undecided.
- Recheck lipids **4–12 weeks** after any change.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- LDL particles enter the artery wall, get oxidized and are eaten by macrophages → foam cells → plaque. **Total LDL exposure over a lifetime** drives risk.
- Statins block HMG-CoA reductase → the liver makes less cholesterol → more LDL receptors pull LDL out of blood. Ezetimibe blocks gut absorption (NPC1L1). PCSK9 inhibitors stop LDL-receptor breakdown.
- Each 1 mmol/L (≈39 mg/dL) drop in LDL lowers major vascular events by about 20%, whatever the drug.

## 🔎 Who needs what

| Group | Therapy |
|---|---|
| Clinical ASCVD | High-intensity statin; add ezetimibe ± PCSK9i if LDL ≥70 (aim <55 if very high risk) |
| LDL ≥190 mg/dL | High-intensity statin; screen relatives |
| Diabetes, 40–75 y | Moderate-intensity; high-intensity if more risk factors |
| 10-y risk ≥20% | High-intensity |
| 10-y risk 7.5%–<20% | Moderate-intensity after shared decision; coronary calcium score if unsure (CAC 0 → may defer) |

## 💊 Management

- **High intensity**: atorvastatin 40–80 mg or rosuvastatin 20–40 mg (≥50% LDL reduction).
- **Muscle symptoms**: most are not caused by the statin. Check CK if severe; stop, let symptoms resolve, then rechallenge with a different statin or lower/alternate-day dose.
- **Triglycerides ≥500 mg/dL** → treat to prevent pancreatitis (fibrate, omega-3, diet, control diabetes and alcohol). Icosapent ethyl lowers events in ASCVD with triglycerides 150–499 on a statin.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Niacin and fibrates added to a statin do **not** reduce cardiovascular events.
- Statins are contraindicated in **pregnancy** and decompensated cirrhosis; stable chronic liver disease is not a contraindication.
- Hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome and cholestasis raise LDL — check TSH before blaming genes.
- Gemfibrozil + statin → high rhabdomyolysis risk; use fenofibrate if a fibrate is needed.
:::
