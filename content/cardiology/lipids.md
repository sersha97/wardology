:::bottom
- **Risk calculator**: the **PREVENT-ASCVD** equations (age 30–79) replace the Pooled Cohort Equations. 10-year risk: **low <3%**, **borderline 3%–<5%**, **intermediate 5%–<10%**, **high ≥10%**.
- **LDL goals are back** (2026): borderline/intermediate **<100 mg/dL**, high risk **<70**, ASCVD at very high risk **<55**.
- **Clinical ASCVD** → high-intensity statin; above goal → add **ezetimibe**, then a **PCSK9 antibody** (or **bempedoic acid** if statin intolerant).
- **LDL ≥190 mg/dL** → high-intensity statin, no calculator; think familial hypercholesterolemia and screen relatives.
- **Start lipid-lowering at age ≥40** with **diabetes, CKD stage ≥3 or HIV**, whatever the LDL or risk score.
- **Lp(a) once in every adult.** **CAC** when risk is borderline/intermediate and the decision is uncertain (men ≥40, women ≥45).
- Recheck lipids **4–12 weeks** after any change.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- LDL particles enter the artery wall, get oxidized and are eaten by macrophages → foam cells → plaque. **Total LDL exposure over a lifetime** drives risk — "lower for longer."
- Statins block HMG-CoA reductase → the liver makes less cholesterol → more LDL receptors pull LDL out of blood. Ezetimibe blocks gut absorption (NPC1L1). PCSK9 antibodies stop LDL-receptor breakdown. Bempedoic acid blocks ATP-citrate lyase upstream of HMG-CoA reductase — it is a prodrug activated in the liver, not muscle, so it rarely causes myalgia.
- Each 1 mmol/L (≈39 mg/dL) drop in LDL lowers major vascular events by about 20%, whatever the drug.

## 🔎 Who needs what (2026)

| Group | Therapy | LDL-C goal |
|---|---|---|
| ASCVD, very high risk | High-intensity statin + ezetimibe ± PCSK9 antibody | **<55 mg/dL** |
| ASCVD, not very high risk | High-intensity statin, add-ons as needed | <70 mg/dL |
| LDL ≥190 mg/dL | High-intensity statin; screen relatives | ≥50% reduction, then goal by risk |
| Diabetes, CKD ≥3 or HIV, age ≥40 | Start lipid-lowering therapy | By risk category |
| PREVENT 10-y ≥10% (high) | Statin | <70 mg/dL |
| PREVENT 5%–<10% (intermediate) | Statin recommended after discussion; CAC if uncertain | <100 mg/dL |
| PREVENT 3%–<5% (borderline) | Statin reasonable; risk enhancers, CAC | <100 mg/dL |

Any coronary calcium supports an LDL goal **<100 mg/dL**, lower with more calcium.

## 💊 Management

- **High intensity**: atorvastatin 40–80 mg or rosuvastatin 20–40 mg (≥50% LDL reduction).
- **Muscle symptoms**: most are not caused by the statin (nocebo). Check **CK and TSH** (hypothyroidism predisposes); stop, let symptoms resolve, then rechallenge with a different statin, a lower dose or alternate-day dosing. True intolerance → ezetimibe, **bempedoic acid** (CLEAR Outcomes: fewer cardiovascular events), PCSK9 antibody.
- **Triglycerides ≥1,000 mg/dL** (≥500 by older cutoffs) → prevent pancreatitis: very-low-fat diet, stop alcohol, control diabetes, **fenofibrate**.
- **Triglycerides 150–499 on a statin with ASCVD (or diabetes + risk factors)** → **icosapent ethyl** (REDUCE-IT). Over-the-counter fish oil and mixed omega-3 products do not reduce events.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Niacin and fibrates added to a statin do **not** reduce cardiovascular events.
- Statins: defer during **conception, pregnancy and lactation**; stable chronic liver disease is not a contraindication; continue during cancer treatment.
- Hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome and cholestasis raise LDL — check TSH before blaming genes.
- Gemfibrozil + statin → high rhabdomyolysis risk; use fenofibrate if a fibrate is needed.
- **Lp(a) is genetic** — one measurement is enough; ≥50 mg/dL (≥125 nmol/L) is a risk enhancer.
:::
