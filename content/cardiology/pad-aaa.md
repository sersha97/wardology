:::bottom
- **ABI ≤0.90 = PAD**; >1.40 = noncompressible vessels → toe-brachial index.
- Claudication → **statin + antiplatelet + supervised exercise** (≥12 weeks); cilostazol for persistent symptoms. Revascularize for lifestyle-limiting symptoms despite therapy or **limb-threatening ischemia**.
- **AAA screen**: one-time ultrasound, **men 65–75 who ever smoked**.
- Repair AAA at **≥5.5 cm (men), ≥5.0 cm (women)**, growth >0.5 cm/6 months, or symptoms.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- PAD is atherosclerosis of the legs: a **marker of systemic vascular risk** — most patients die of MI or stroke, not limb loss.
- AAA is degeneration of the aortic wall (smoking is the strongest risk factor); rupture risk rises sharply with diameter.

## 🔎 Classify the leg

| Presentation | Clue | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Asymptomatic | Low ABI | Risk factor control |
| Claudication | Reproducible calf pain with walking | Exercise, statin, antiplatelet |
| Chronic limb-threatening ischemia | Rest pain, ulcers, gangrene | Vascular referral, revascularization |
| **Acute limb ischemia** | **6 Ps**: pain, pallor, pulselessness, paresthesia, paralysis, poikilothermia | **Heparin + emergency revascularization** |

## 💊 Drugs that help — and don't

- ✅ Statin, aspirin or clopidogrel, low-dose rivaroxaban + aspirin (selected), cilostazol (no heart failure).
- ❌ Pentoxifylline, full-dose warfarin, chelation.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Women who never smoked: **do not** screen for AAA.
- AAA surveillance: 3.0–3.9 cm every 3 years; 4.0–4.9 cm yearly; 5.0–5.4 cm every 6 months.
- Diabetic with "normal" ABI >1.4 still may have PAD (calcified arteries).
:::
