:::bottom
- **Confirm** office readings with **home or ambulatory** monitoring before labeling hypertension.
- **Goal <130/80 mm Hg** for most adults.
- **Stage 2 (≥140/90)** → lifestyle **plus two first-line drugs**, ideally one pill.
- First-line classes: **ACE inhibitor/ARB, dihydropyridine CCB, thiazide-type diuretic** ("A-C-D").
- **Never** combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB.
- Severe BP **without organ damage** → oral drugs and follow-up, not the ED.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Blood pressure = cardiac output × systemic vascular resistance. In most adults, **primary (essential) hypertension** comes from salt sensitivity, arterial stiffening and an overactive renin–angiotensin and sympathetic system.
- High pressure injures the **brain, heart, kidneys, eyes and arteries** silently for years — stroke and heart failure are the biggest payoffs of treatment.
- About 10% have a **secondary cause** — think of it when hypertension is resistant, starts suddenly, begins before age 30, or comes with hypokalemia.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| Category | Office BP (mm Hg) |
|---|---|
| Normal | <120 / <80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 / <80 |
| Stage 1 | 130–139 / 80–89 |
| Stage 2 | ≥140 / ≥90 |

- Use the average of ≥2 readings on ≥2 occasions, seated, arm at heart level, correct cuff size.
- **White-coat**: high in office, normal at home. **Masked**: normal in office, high at home — same risk as sustained hypertension.
- Baseline tests: electrolytes, creatinine/eGFR, glucose or A1c, lipids, TSH, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, ECG.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Stage 1
Lifestyle for all. Add **one** first-line drug if the patient has clinical CVD, diabetes, CKD, or high estimated 10-year risk — otherwise recheck in 3–6 months.
=== Stage 2
Lifestyle **plus two first-line drugs from different classes**, preferably a **single-pill combination** (for example ARB + amlodipine, or ACE inhibitor + chlorthalidone). Recheck monthly until at goal.
=== Resistant
Above goal on three drugs (including a diuretic) at full doses → check adherence, home readings, NSAIDs/decongestants/alcohol, sleep apnea and **primary aldosteronism**; add **spironolactone** as the fourth drug.
:::

- Lifestyle that works: weight loss (≈1 mm Hg per kg), DASH diet, sodium <1.5–2.3 g/day, potassium-rich food, aerobic exercise, alcohol ≤1–2 drinks/day.
- Compelling indications: **ACE inhibitor/ARB** for albuminuric CKD and diabetes with albuminuria; **beta-blocker** after MI or with HFrEF.

:::warn
Immediate-release nifedipine and IV drugs for asymptomatic severe hypertension cause strokes and MIs. Organ damage — not the number — defines an emergency.
:::

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Stage 2 → **two drugs at once**, single pill. Beta-blockers are **not** first-line without another indication.
- Hypokalemia + hypertension → screen for **primary aldosteronism** (aldosterone-to-renin ratio). Current advice is to screen all patients with resistant hypertension.
- Young woman with resistant hypertension and an abdominal bruit → **fibromuscular dysplasia**.
- Thiazides cause hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hyperuricemia (gout) and hypercalcemia; ACE inhibitors cause cough and angioedema; dihydropyridine CCBs cause ankle edema.
:::
