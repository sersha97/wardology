:::bottom
- Severe lesion + **symptoms** → intervene.
- Asymptomatic severe **AR**: surgery if **EF ≤55%** or **LVESD >50 mm**.
- Asymptomatic severe primary **MR**: surgery if **EF ≤60%** or **LVESD ≥40 mm** (repair > replace).
- Rheumatic **MS** + AF → **warfarin** (not a DOAC); balloon commissurotomy if anatomy favorable.
:::

## 🔎 Murmurs at a glance

| Lesion | Murmur | Clue |
|---|---|---|
| **Aortic regurgitation** | Early decrescendo diastolic, LSB, leaning forward | Wide pulse pressure, bounding pulses |
| **Mitral regurgitation** | Holosystolic apex → axilla | Louder with handgrip |
| **Mitral stenosis** | Opening snap + diastolic rumble at apex | Rheumatic history, AF, pregnancy decompensation |
| Mitral valve prolapse | Midsystolic click + late murmur | Click earlier with Valsalva |

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **Chronic regurgitation** = volume overload → the LV dilates and compensates for years, then fails silently. That is why we operate on numbers (EF, LV size) before symptoms.
- **Mitral stenosis** = obstruction to LV filling → high left atrial pressure, AF, pulmonary edema. Tachycardia (shorter diastole) and pregnancy (more volume) unmask it.

## 💊 Medical facts

- Vasodilators treat hypertension in AR but do not delay surgery.
- Secondary (functional) MR from LV dilation → GDMT first, then transcatheter edge-to-edge repair in selected patients.
- **DOACs are fine** for AF with AS, MR, AR and bioprosthetic valves (>3 months) — the exceptions are **moderate–severe rheumatic MS and mechanical valves**.

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- Acute severe AR (endocarditis, dissection) → emergency surgery; avoid beta-blockers (they block compensatory tachycardia).
- Bicuspid aortic valve → image the aorta; repair at ≥5.5 cm (≥4.5 cm if already having valve surgery).
:::
