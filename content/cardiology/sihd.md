:::bottom
- **Typical angina** = substernal + exertional + relieved by rest/nitroglycerin (3 of 3).
- Intermediate risk, age <65, no known CAD → **CCTA** (or stress test). Known CAD or older/heavy calcium → **stress imaging**.
- Everyone with CAD: **statin, antiplatelet, BP/diabetes control, exercise, smoking cessation**. Revascularization is for refractory symptoms or high-risk anatomy (left main).
- **No PDE-5 inhibitors with nitrates** — ever.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- A fixed plaque limits flow when demand rises (exercise, emotion) → **supply–demand ischemia** that resolves with rest.
- Plaque **rupture** with thrombus turns stable angina into **acute coronary syndrome** — a different disease with different tests (troponins) and urgency.

## 🔎 Which test?

| Situation | Test |
|---|---|
| Low risk, age <40, atypical pain | Often none |
| Intermediate risk, <65 y, no known CAD | **Coronary CT angiography** or stress test |
| Can exercise + normal ECG | Exercise ECG acceptable |
| Known CAD, LBBB, paced, heavy calcium | **Stress imaging** (echo, SPECT, PET, CMR) |
| High-risk noninvasive result or refractory symptoms | Invasive angiography |

## 💊 Medical therapy

:::tabs
=== Prevent events
- High-intensity statin (LDL goal <70, or <55 if very high risk); add ezetimibe → PCSK9 inhibitor.
- Aspirin 81 mg (or clopidogrel).
- SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 RA with diabetes.
=== Relieve angina
- **Beta-blocker or calcium channel blocker** first.
- Long-acting nitrate or **ranolazine** as add-ons.
- Sublingual nitroglycerin for attacks.
:::

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **ISCHEMIA trial**: in stable CAD with moderate–severe ischemia, routine invasive strategy did not reduce death or MI versus optimal medical therapy.
- Left main stenosis ≥50% and multivessel CAD with diabetes → **CABG**.
- Erectile dysfunction is a vascular risk marker — and a nitrate contraindicates PDE-5 inhibitors.
:::
