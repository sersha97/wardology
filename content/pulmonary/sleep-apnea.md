:::bottom
- Snoring + witnessed apneas + sleepiness → sleep testing.
- **Home sleep apnea test** for uncomplicated adults with high probability; **in-lab PSG** for cardiopulmonary, neuromuscular, opioid or hypoventilation concerns.
- AHI **5–14 mild, 15–29 moderate, ≥30 severe**.
- **CPAP first-line** for moderate–severe OSA or any OSA with sleepiness.
- **Weight loss** for everyone with obesity.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- During sleep, pharyngeal muscle tone falls; a crowded airway (obesity, large neck, retrognathia, big tonsils) collapses.
- Each apnea → hypoxemia, CO₂ rise, sympathetic surge and arousal → fragmented sleep, **hypertension**, AF and stroke risk.

## 💊 Management

| Therapy | Best for |
|---|---|
| CPAP / auto-PAP | Moderate–severe, sleepy patients (adherence ≥4 h/night) |
| Mandibular advancement device | Mild–moderate, or CPAP intolerance |
| Weight loss (diet, GLP-1-based drugs, bariatric surgery) | OSA with obesity |
| Hypoglossal nerve stimulation | Moderate–severe, CPAP-intolerant, BMI criteria |
| Positional therapy | Supine-predominant OSA |

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **Obesity hypoventilation**: BMI ≥30 + **daytime PaCO₂ ≥45** (high bicarbonate) → in-lab study, often bilevel PAP.
- Avoid benzodiazepines, opioids and alcohol before sleep.
- Commercial drivers with suspected OSA need evaluation before certification.
:::
