:::bottom
- **Incidental** nodule, age ≥35 → **Fleischner** rules; **screening** CT → **Lung-RADS**.
- Solid **<6 mm**, low risk → no follow-up. **6–8 mm** → CT at 6–12 months. **>8 mm** → CT at 3 months, PET-CT or biopsy.
- Solid nodule stable for **2 years** = benign. Subsolid → follow up to 5 years.
- **Calcification** in a benign pattern (central, diffuse, laminated, popcorn) → benign.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Most small nodules are granulomas or intrapulmonary lymph nodes.
- Cancer risk rises with **size, spiculation, upper-lobe location, growth, smoking, age and emphysema**.
- Adenocarcinoma can start as a **ground-glass** nodule that grows slowly over years.

## 🔎 Risk and next step

| Nodule | Next step |
|---|---|
| Solid >8 mm, low–moderate probability | PET-CT or CT at 3 months |
| Solid >8 mm, high probability | Tissue (bronchoscopic or CT-guided) or resection |
| Pure ground-glass <6 mm | No routine follow-up |
| Pure ground-glass ≥6 mm | CT at 6–12 months, then every 2 years to 5 years |

## 🎓 Board corner

:::board
- **PET-CT** is unreliable for nodules <8 mm and for ground-glass lesions (false negatives); false positives with infection and granulomas.
- Fleischner rules do **not** apply to patients with known cancer, immunosuppression or lung cancer screening.
- Lung cancer screening: annual LDCT, ages **50–80**, **≥20 pack-years**, current or quit within **15 years**.
:::
